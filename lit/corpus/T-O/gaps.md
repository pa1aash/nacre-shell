# T-O gaps: anodic stability of the anions of other candidate biominerals (P0-W1.4)

Purpose: evidence for one sourced Outlook sentence. Rows TO-001 to TO-034 (lit/corpus/T-O/rows.csv).
All potentials are vs Li+/Li. Computed windows are 0 K thermodynamic limits; the sources themselves
note that kinetics can shift the observed onset (TO-006, TO-014).

## Targets

- **1. Phosphate, calcium phosphates, hydroxyapatite.**
  - Searched: arxiv "electrochemical stability window solid electrolyte first principles Li3PO4"; openalex "Li3PO4 thin film electrochemical stability window linear sweep voltammetry oxidation"; openalex "hydroxyapatite lithium ion battery cathode coating electrolyte high voltage"; europepmc "LiCoPO4 high voltage olivine electrolyte oxidation ... OPEN_ACCESS:y". Direct DOIs: Lim/Siegel 2019, Xiao 2019 Joule, Zhu/He/Mo 2016 JMCA, Binninger 2020, Nolan 2018 Joule, Jin/Cui 2017, Yu 1997, Cai 2023.
  - Found: computed Li3PO4 window 0.69..4.21 V (TO-001), V_ox 4.22 V (TO-007), 0.7..4.2 V cited from Richards 2016 (TO-034); LiPO3 2.48..4.99 V (TO-003); LiZr2(PO4)3 4.52 V with O2 among the products (TO-011); oxidation pathway through the O 2p states giving peroxide, superoxide or O2 (TO-012); orthophosphates have the lowest median V_ox of the phosphates (TO-013); LiPON computed onset 1.07 V and products at 5 V (TO-015, TO-016, TO-018); LiPON stoichiometry window 0.00..4.11 V (TO-019); experimental LiPON window to about 5.0-5.5 V (TO-020 compiled, TO-021 abstract); Li3PO4 converting to LiPO3 under high voltage (TO-022, abstract); olivine LiCoPO4 4.19 V and LiNiPO4 4.22 V (TO-009, TO-010); LATP 4.31 V (TO-017).
  - Not found: no oxidation onset or computed window for any calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite, beta-TCP, brushite) was found. The only hydroxyapatite row is a separator-cell LSV (TO-033), which is a proxy because the electrolyte and separator dominate it. No open source separates the transition-metal redox from phosphate-anion oxidation in olivine cathodes. Classic sources such as Padhi 1997 were not tried, because they are paywalled and give no anion-oxidation number. Paywalled or no open copy: Zhu/He/Mo 2015 (10.1021/acsami.5b07517) and Richards 2016 (10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b04082); both were tried again this session, both "no retrievable copy", the same dead end as T-E. Cai 2023 (10.1002/adsu.202300392) has no retrievable copy, so it is abstract only. Yu 1997 (10.1149/1.1837443) is paywalled, abstract only, and the abstract gives no scan rate.

- **2. Phosphate surface layers on aluminium.**
  - Searched: openalex "aluminum current collector phosphate passivation LiTFSI anodic dissolution", "phosphate conversion coating aluminum current collector lithium ion anodic", "AlPO4 coating cathode high voltage electrochemical stability first principles"; two open reviews (Shi 2023, Han 2023) read.
  - Found (secondary only): an AlPO4 layer on Al shows no dissolution peak in CV and a low CA leakage current (TO-031). A fluorinated cyclic phosphate solvent (TFEP) decomposes on Al to an organic fluorophosphate and AlF3 (TO-032), i.e. the phosphate is consumed.
  - Not found: no primary AlPO4-on-Al or phosphate-conversion-on-Al study with printed potentials and electrolyte was read. Gao et al. 2018 (10.1016/j.mtener.2017.12.001) has no open copy and no abstract. Markovsky et al. 2010 (10.1149/1.3294774) is paywalled. Zheng et al. 2020 Nat. Energy (10.1038/s41560-020-0601-1) is paywalled with no abstract. Shi 2023 numbers its references out of step with its text, so the AlPO4 original is uncertain (TO-031 notes). The windows computed for AlPO4 itself are in Zhu 2016 Table 1 and SI, but no AlPO4 oxidation limit was printed in the pages read. Li3PO4 or AlPO4 coatings on cathodes in liquid electrolyte with a printed onset were not found.

- **3. Phosphate-containing electrolyte additives.**
  - Searched: europepmc "tris(trimethylsilyl) phosphate oxidation potential high voltage cathode electrolyte additive OPEN_ACCESS:y"; openalex "phosphate ester electrolyte additive oxidation potential DFT high voltage cathode electrolyte interphase", "tris(trimethylsilyl) phosphate additive high voltage", "lithium difluorophosphate LiPO2F2 additive oxidation aluminum".
  - Found: TMSPa is not oxidised and leaves no oxidation-derived layer on HE-NCM up to 4.7 V; it acts as a chemical HF scavenger, and earlier literature disagrees on its oxidative stability (TO-023, TO-024). The phosphite TMSPi shows extra CV current above 4.5 V on carbon (TO-025).
  - Not found: no computed or measured oxidation potential of TMSP, triphenyl phosphate, trimethyl phosphate or LiPO2F2 was read. Yan 2013 (10.1016/j.jpowsour.2013.10.037) and Delp 2016 (10.1016/j.electacta.2016.05.100) are paywalled with no abstract. Han 2016 (10.1016/j.electacta.2016.08.131) gave no abstract. Some works describe TMSP as sacrificial (Zhang 2014, the title cited in Gueguen 2019); this was not checked against an original.

- **4. Oxalate, sulfate, silicate.**
  - Searched: openalex "lithium oxalate Li2C2O4 sacrificial cathode additive decomposition potential CO2", "Li2SO4 electrochemical stability oxidation sulfate solid electrolyte high voltage"; arxiv "cathode coating screening high-throughput oxidation limit sulfate silicate phosphate lithium"; semanticscholar "lithium silicate Li4SiO4 coating cathode electrochemical stability window oxidation" (rate limited, no relevant hit).
  - Found: oxalate is oxidised quantitatively to CO2 on a plateau around 4.7 V in LP57 at C/10 (TO-026), with 4.6-4.7 V cited from earlier work (TO-027) and a negligible electrolyte CO2 background up to 5 V (TO-028). Li2SO4 is in the computed group with V_ox above 4 V, but only as a qualitative statement (TO-004). A Li2SO4 surface layer is still detected by XPS after charge-discharge (TO-030; potential range not printed). Silicates: Li4SiO4 0.26..3.41 V (TO-002), LiAlSiO4 4.09 V (TO-008), and the qualitative trend that Li2SiO3 has a higher V_ox (TO-005).
  - Not found: no number for the Li2SO4 or Li2CO3 oxidation limit. Both are in Lim/Siegel SI Table S2, and the SI was not retrieved. No calcium oxalate, calcium sulfate (gypsum or anhydrite) or calcium silicate value of any kind was found. No sulfate-based solid electrolyte window was found.

- **5. Same-source contrast of Li2CO3, Li3PO4, Li2SO4 and Li4SiO4.**
  - Found: Lim/Siegel 2019 is the only source read that covers all four. In its main text it prints Li3PO4 0.69..4.21 V and Li4SiO4 0.26..3.41 V (TO-001, TO-002). It names Li2CO3 and Li2SO4 only as having V_ox above 4 V (TO-004).
  - Not found: numeric Li2CO3 and Li2SO4 limits from the same source (SI Table S2 not retrieved). For Li2CO3 the thread relies on the existing TI and TK rows (for example, the 3.82 V thermodynamic value discussed in TK-060 and TK-063), which this thread does not duplicate.

## What the sources support / do not support

- **Phosphate (PO4 3-).** Supported: computed windows place the oxidation limit of crystalline Li3PO4 at about 4.2 V vs Li+/Li, which is inside the 4-5 V cathode range (TO-001, TO-007, TO-034). The computed oxidation route is removal of O 2p electrons with O2 release and condensation to pyro- or metaphosphate (TO-011, TO-012, TO-022). Condensed phosphates (LiPO3) reach about 5 V (TO-003). Measured LiPON windows reach 5.0-5.5 V, but its computed thermodynamic onset is about 1.1 V (TO-015, TO-020, TO-021). The measured stability is therefore kinetic, not intrinsic. Not supported: any statement about calcium phosphate or hydroxyapatite anodic stability in a Li-ion electrolyte. No onset was found, and the only HAP row is a separator-cell proxy (TO-033). The sources also do not support calling orthophosphate thermodynamically stable above about 4.2 V.
- **Phosphate on aluminium and phosphate additives.** Supported: secondary reports of AlPO4 layers on Al without an anodic dissolution peak (TO-031). A phosphate solvent decomposes at Al to a fluorophosphate and AlF3 layer (TO-032). TMSPa is not oxidised on HE-NCM up to 4.7 V and acts chemically as an HF scavenger (TO-023, TO-024). Not supported: a primary measurement of a phosphate layer on Al with stated potential and electrolyte, or any oxidation potential of a phosphate additive.
- **Oxalate (C2O4 2-).** Supported: the oxalate anion is a sacrificial oxidant that is oxidised to CO2 at about 4.6-4.7 V vs Li+/Li (TO-026, TO-027). This is the opposite of anodic stability. Not supported: any calcium oxalate value. The Li2C2O4 value is a lithium-salt analogue.
- **Sulfate (SO4 2-).** Supported only qualitatively: Li2SO4 is computed to have an oxidation limit above 4 V (TO-004), and a Li2SO4 surface layer persists on a Li-rich cathode after charge-discharge, with the potential range not printed (TO-030). Not supported: a numeric sulfate oxidation limit or any calcium sulfate value.
- **Silicate (SiO4 4-, SiO3 2-).** Supported: the orthosilicate Li4SiO4 has a computed oxidation limit of 3.41 V, below 4 V (TO-002). LiAlSiO4 is computed at 4.09 V (TO-008), and Li-poorer silicates are computed higher (TO-005). Not supported: any calcium silicate value, or a silicate stable above about 4.1 V in these sources.
- **Comparison with carbonate.** In Lim/Siegel's main text, Li2CO3, Li3PO4 and Li2SO4 share the qualitative group "V_ox above 4 V", and Li4SiO4 falls below 4 V (TO-004, TO-002). On that evidence alone, orthophosphate is not clearly more anodically stable than carbonate. A sourced ranking needs the SI Table S2 numbers, which are a gap.
