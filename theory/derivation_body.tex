\section{Regimes and what each link must achieve}\label{th:sec:regimes}

\textit{Physics.} The proposed mechanism is assembled in one chemical environment and must
survive in another. The template is anchored, binds Ca$^{2+}$ and templates the mineral while
the electrode is a water-processed, alkaline slurry at room temperature (\Rs). The finished
interface then sits in a non-aqueous, fluorine-free electrolyte with the Al collector held at
the cathode's anodic potential (\Rc). The two regimes have different solvents, different
reference states and different corrosion modes, so a relation is meaningful only in the regime
whose reference states it uses. Every relation below is therefore tagged with its regime, and
none is carried across without saying so.

Table~\ref{th:tab:regimes} states, for each link, the necessary condition the link must meet
in its regime. These are directions of inequalities, not thresholds: the P0 thresholds, margins and
decision rules are in \texttt{theory/thresholds.md}. Meeting them makes the mechanism thermodynamically possible under the
stated framing; it does not show that a barrier works, and no rate, capacity or device statement
follows from any of them.

\section{Adhesion in water}\label{th:sec:adhesion}

\textit{Physics.} In the slurry the template never meets a dry oxide. The hydroxylated
Al$_2$O$_3$/AlOOH surface is covered by hydrogen-bonded water, and the polymer is hydrated too.
A catechol--oxide contact can only form by displacing that water, so the relevant comparison is
between two states that actually compete in the slurry: the oxide and polymer in contact, and
the oxide and polymer each wetted by water. The dry (vacuum) work of adhesion compares the
contact with two bare surfaces, a state that never occurs in \Rs, and is therefore only a
comparator.

The reversible work per unit area to separate a contact between phases $A$ and $B$ inside a
medium $M$ is the Dupr\'e work $W=\gamma_{AM}+\gamma_{BM}-\gamma_{AB}$: the two new $A$--$M$ and
$B$--$M$ interfaces are created and the $A$--$B$ interface is destroyed. With $A$ the oxide,
$B$ the polymer and $M$ water,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dupre}
\Wwet=\gow+\gpw-\gop \un{J\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
Units: each term is an interfacial free energy, so $\Wwet$ is in J\,m$^{-2}$. Writing every
in-water energy through the dry works of each surface with water,
$\gow=\go+\gw-W_\mathrm{ow}$ and $\gpw=\gp+\gw-W_\mathrm{pw}$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dupre-dry}
\Wwet=\Wdry+2\gw-W_\mathrm{ow}-W_\mathrm{pw},\qquad \Wdry=\go+\gp-\gop \un{J\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{th:eq:dupre-dry} is the water-competition statement in one line: a large dry
adhesion is offset by the works $W_\mathrm{ow}$ and $W_\mathrm{pw}$ with which water already
binds each surface. A hydrophilic, hydroxylated oxide and a hydrophilic polymer bind water
strongly, so $\Wwet$ can be small or negative even when $\Wdry$ is large. The bare-oxide control
has $\Wwet=0$ by construction and fixes the zero of the scale.

The interfacial energies of Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dupre} are not computed separately. The potential
of mean force (PMF) of a template fragment along a separation coordinate in explicit water gives
the free-energy difference between the separated and bound states, $\Delta F_\mathrm{PMF}$
(J per fragment), which already contains the displacement penalty and the entropy of the
released water. Normalising by the contact area,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:pmf}
\Wwet=\frac{\Delta F_\mathrm{PMF}}{A_\mathrm{c}} \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
with units J/m$^2$. Two conditions make Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:pmf} meaningful: the PMF must reach a
plateau at large separation (both surfaces fully rewetted), and $A_\mathrm{c}$ must be defined
once for all fragments, because the ranking across binders depends on it (open question Q5).
C1 requires $\Wwet>0$ beyond its uncertainty on the populated oxide terminations.

\section{The sequestration cycle and the enrichment factor}\label{th:sec:sequestration}

\textit{Physics.} The template can raise the local Ca$^{2+}$ concentration only if it holds
Ca$^{2+}$ more strongly than water does. The question is a competition between two ligand
environments for the same ion: the hydration shell in bulk solution and the coordinating groups
of the template. The free energy must therefore start from hydrated Ca$^{2+}$ at the regime
activity, include the cost of preparing the binding site at the regime pH, and end in a
well-defined local state.

\paragraph{Cycle and standard states.} Let the coordinating group be an acid L--H that binds
Ca$^{2+}$ only when deprotonated. The cycle is
\begin{align}
\text{(i)}\quad &\text{L--H (layer)} \;\to\; \text{L}^{-}\text{(layer)} + \text{H}^{+}\text{(aq)},
&&\Delta G_\mathrm{i}=-\Rg T\ln\alpha \un{J\,mol^{-1}}\nonumber\\
\text{(ii)}\quad &\text{Ca}^{2+}\text{(aq, bulk)} + \text{L}^{-} \;\to\; \text{Ca}^{2+}\!\cdot\text{L}^{-}\text{(layer)},
&&\Delta G_\mathrm{ii}=\DGsite \un{J\,mol^{-1}}\nonumber
\end{align}
with $\alpha=1/\big(1+10^{\,\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{pH}}\big)$ (dimensionless).
Step (i) is the free energy of finding the site deprotonated when the solution fixes the proton
activity: $\alpha$ is the deprotonated fraction at the regime pH, and $-\Rg T\ln\alpha\ge0$. Step
(ii) is the transfer of one Ca$^{2+}$ from bulk solution into the binding layer. Summing,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:cycle}
\DGseq=\DGsite+\Rg T\ln\!\big(1+10^{\,\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{pH}}\big) \un{J\,mol^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
Units: $\Rg T$ is in J\,mol$^{-1}$ and the logarithm is of a pure number. The correction vanishes
as $\mathrm{pH}-\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}\to\infty$, equals $\Rg T\ln2$ at $\mathrm{pH}=\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}$, and
grows as the site becomes protonated. Both $\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}$ and the activity coefficients are
taken at the regime ionic strength. This is why catechol, largely protonated near neutral pH,
must be tested rather than assumed (design flag 4).

Step (ii) fixes the standard states. Let $w(\mathbf r)$ be the PMF of one Ca$^{2+}$ relative to
bulk solution (J per ion, $w\to0$ far from the template), computed with counter-ions and water
explicit. In the dilute limit the equilibrium concentration is $c(\mathbf r)=\cb\,\e^{-w(\mathbf r)/\kB T}$.
Assigning to each site a volume $\Vsite$ (the binding-layer volume per site) and defining
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:site}
\DGsite\equiv-\Rg T\ln\!\Big[\frac{1}{\Vsite}\int_{\mathrm{site}}\e^{-w(\mathbf r)/\kB T}\,\dd^3 r\Big] \un{J\,mol^{-1}}
\end{equation}
(the bracket is a ratio of volumes, so it is a pure number) makes the transfer free energy a
partition between two concentration scales: the bulk at the regime activity on the solution
side, and the mean concentration in the site volume on the layer side. The conventional binding
free energy to a 1\,mol-per-volume standard state $c^{\circ}$ differs from it by the
standard-state correction
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:stdstate}
\Delta G^{\circ}_\mathrm{bind}=\DGsite-\Rg T\ln\!\big(\NA c^{\circ}\Vsite\big) \un{J\,mol^{-1}},
\end{equation}
where $\NA c^{\circ}\Vsite$ is dimensionless. A literature binding constant can therefore be
compared with $\DGsite$ only after Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:stdstate}, and only with the same $\Vsite$.
The choice of $\Vsite$ shifts $\DGsite$ by $\Rg T\ln(\Vsite'/\Vsite)$, so it is fixed once
(open question Q4).

\paragraph{Activity versus concentration.} Equation~\eqref{th:eq:site} defines a concentration
ratio. Because $w$ contains every interaction of the ion with its surroundings, the
electrochemical potential of Ca$^{2+}$ is uniform at equilibrium (Section~\ref{th:sec:supersaturation}):
the ion in the layer carries the same chemical potential as in bulk, and the whole enrichment
sits in its local standard potential, equivalently in a local activity coefficient
$\gamma_\mathrm{loc}=\gamma_\mathrm{bulk}/\Eenr$. $\Eenr$ is therefore a concentration ratio and
never an activity ratio. Contract v1 used a \emph{local-ideal} bookkeeping that gave the
enriched ions the bulk standard state; Section~\ref{th:sec:template} shows that it is inconsistent
at equilibrium and replaces it.

\paragraph{Enrichment and its validity range.} In the dilute limit with non-interacting sites,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:enr}
\Eenr\equiv\frac{\cl}{\cb}=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\DGseq}{\Rg T}\Big) \un{1}.
\end{equation}
$\DGseq<0$ gives enrichment ($\Eenr>1$); $\DGseq>0$ gives depletion: the template is then a worse
ligand than water. Equation~\eqref{th:eq:enr} holds when (a) Ca$^{2+}$ is dilute in bulk, (b)
the sites are far from saturation, (c) one ion binds per site and sites do not interact, and (d)
the non-specific (ionic-strength) activity coefficient is the same in the layer as in bulk, so
that $\Eenr$ is fixed by the PMF alone. Condition (b) is quantified next.

\paragraph{Saturation bound.} The layer has $\Gs$ sites per unit area in a thickness $\ds$, so
its Ca$^{2+}$ content cannot exceed $\cmax=\Gs/\ds$ (mol\,m$^{-2}$ over m gives mol\,m$^{-3}$).
For independent, equivalent sites (Langmuir), the occupied fraction is
$\Theta=x/(1+x)$ with $x=\Eenr\cb/\cmax$, and $\cl=\cmax\Theta$:
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:langmuir}
\cl=\frac{\Eenr\cb}{1+\Eenr\cb/\cmax}\le\min\big(\Eenr\cb,\ \cmax\big) \un{mol\,m^{-3}}.
\end{equation}
The dilute result \eqref{th:eq:enr} is the limit $x\ll1$; at $x\gg1$ the enrichment saturates at
$\cmax/\cb$ however negative $\DGseq$ is. A strongly binding but sparse template is therefore
bounded by its site density, not by its binding free energy. Electrostatic coupling between
charged sites, which Langmuir ignores, is open question Q3.

\paragraph{Occupancy and surface excess.} The same closure gives the fraction of occupied sites
and the Gibbs relative excess of Ca$^{2+}$ at the template--liquid interface, the bound amount
per area less the amount the layer volume would hold at the bulk concentration:
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:excess}
\Theta=\frac{x}{1+x}=\frac{\cl}{\cmax},\qquad x=\frac{\Eenr\cb}{\cmax} \un{1},\qquad
\GCa=\Gs\Theta-\ds\cb \un{mol\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
$x$ is the occupancy odds of a site at the regime activity. $\GCa>0$ for net binding and
$\GCa<0$ for a template that repels Ca$^{2+}$. These, not a local supersaturation, are what
C2 hands to C3 (Section~\ref{th:sec:template}).

\section{The driving force at the template}\label{th:sec:supersaturation}

\textit{Physics.} A nucleus is built from ions taken out of the solution, and the free energy it
gains per formula unit is the chemical potential those ions carry minus that of the solid. The
template sits in an open interfacial region that exchanges ions with the slurry. At equilibrium
nothing can be gained by moving an ion between the bulk and the layer, so every ion carries the
same electrochemical potential everywhere: the template can concentrate ions, but it cannot make
them carry more free energy. The driving force at the template is therefore the bulk one. The
algebra below shows this for the two ways a template enriches Ca$^{2+}$, specific binding and a
mean electrostatic potential.

\paragraph{One definition.} Throughout,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:omega}
\Om=\frac{\mathrm{IAP}}{\Ksp}=\frac{\aCa\,\aCO}{\Ksp} \un{1},\qquad \Dmu=\kB T\ln\Om \un{J},
\end{equation}
with $\Ksp$ the solubility product of the registered framing (ACC or calcite), $\Dmu$ the
chemical-potential drop per CaCO$_3$ formula unit on crystallisation, and $\Vm$ the molar volume per
CaCO$_3$ formula unit. Units: activities and $\Ksp$ are pure numbers on the molal scale; $\kB T$ is
in J. Nucleation requires $\Om>1$. This is the charter's convention (F3): its $S$ is this $\Om$.
The per-ion square root $\Om^{1/2}$ with a factor $2\kB T$ is the same statement and is not used,
so that no factor of two can be lost between lanes.

\paragraph{Structural water (Q13).} For a hydrated framing CaCO$_3\cdot n$H$_2$O the ion activity
product is $\aCa\aCO a_\mathrm{w}^{n}$, with $\Ksp$ of the same framing. The screen keeps
$\mathrm{IAP}=\aCa\aCO$. In the slurry $a_\mathrm{w}=1$ and the two agree. At a drying state
$a_\mathrm{w}<1$, and the anhydrous form overstates $\Om$ by the factor $a_\mathrm{w}^{-n}>1$: the
drying states are optimistic, biased toward a larger driving force. The screen therefore reports
$a_\mathrm{w}$ at every drying state and, where $n$ is supplied, the hydrated ratio as a labelled
sensitivity (\texttt{Omega\_bulk\_hydrated}); it never decides a verdict (cp2 fix F7).

\paragraph{Equilibrium of the open interfacial region.} Write the electrochemical potential of ion
$i$ (charge number $z_i$) at position $\mathbf r$ as
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:echem}
\tilde\mu_i(\mathbf r)=\mu_i^{\circ}+w_i^\mathrm{sp}(\mathbf r)+\kB T\ln\frac{\gamma_i(\mathbf r)\,m_i(\mathbf r)}{m^{\circ}}+z_ie\psi(\mathbf r) \un{J},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_i^{\circ}$ is the bulk standard potential, $w_i^\mathrm{sp}$ the specific (non-electrostatic)
interaction with the template ($w^\mathrm{sp}\to0$ in bulk), $\gamma_i$ the non-specific activity
coefficient, $m_i$ the molality and $\psi$ the mean electrostatic potential ($\psi\to0$ in bulk).
Equilibrium of the region with the slurry, which acts as a reservoir, is the statement
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:uniform}
\tilde\mu_i(\mathbf r)=\tilde\mu_{i,\mathrm{bulk}}\quad\text{for every ion }i\text{ and every }\mathbf r.
\end{equation}
Solving Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:echem} for $m_i$ gives the local enrichment,
$m_i(\mathbf r)/m_{i,\mathrm{bulk}}=(\gamma_{i,\mathrm{bulk}}/\gamma_i)\exp[-(w_i^\mathrm{sp}+z_ie\psi)/\kB T]$:
the Boltzmann factor of Section~\ref{th:sec:sequestration} is a \emph{consequence} of
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:uniform}, not an exception to it.

The driving force for adding one formula unit to a nucleus at $\mathbf r$ is
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dmu-uniform}
\Dmu(\mathbf r)=\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca}(\mathbf r)+\tilde\mu_{\mathrm{CO_3}}(\mathbf r)-\mu_\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}
=\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca,bulk}+\tilde\mu_\mathrm{CO_3,bulk}-\mu_\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}=\kB T\ln\Omb \un{J}.
\end{equation}
It does not depend on $\mathbf r$: it is the same at a binding site, in the diffuse layer and in
the bulk.

\paragraph{(i) Specific binding.} At a site, $w_\mathrm{Ca}^\mathrm{sp}$ adds $\DGseq/\NA$ per ion to the
standard part of $\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca}$ (a lowering when $\DGseq<0$) (with the site-volume standard state of
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:site}) and raises the concentration term by $\kB T\ln\Eenr=-\DGseq/\NA$. The two
changes are equal and opposite, which is why $\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca}$ stays at its bulk value. The
local enrichment $\cl/\cb=\Eenr=\exp(-\DGseq/\Rg T)$ therefore changes the driving force by
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:no-gain}
\Dmu_\mathrm{site}-\Dmu_\mathrm{bulk}=\underbrace{\kB T\ln\Eenr}_{\text{concentration}}+\underbrace{\DGseq/\NA}_{\text{standard potential}}=0 \un{J}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(ii) A mean potential.} At an anionic template ($\psi<0$) Ca$^{2+}$ is enriched by
$\e^{-2e\psi/\kB T}$ and CO$_3^{2-}$ depleted by $\e^{+2e\psi/\kB T}$. Even counting concentrations
alone, the ion product is unchanged, $(\aCa\aCO)_\mathrm{local}=(\aCa\aCO)_\mathrm{bulk}$, and in
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dmu-uniform} the potential enters as $(z_\mathrm{Ca}+z_\mathrm{CO_3})e\psi=0$
because the solid is neutral. A mean potential changes neither $\Om$ nor $\Dmu$. This settles
Q2: carbonate needs no separate treatment, and holding it at its bulk activity (the v1 choice) is
not the issue; crediting the enriched Ca$^{2+}$ was.

\paragraph{Result.} At equilibrium the nucleus at the template is driven by $\kB T\ln\Omb$,
evaluated at the regime activities. The template changes the barrier only through the
interfacial energies: the diffuse-layer free energy is part of $\gsl$ and $\gnl$, and the specific
binding enters $\gsl$ through the Gibbs adsorption equation (Section~\ref{th:sec:template}).
The local-ideal ratio $\Omega_\mathrm{li}=(\cl/\cb)\Omb$, with
$\Dmu_\mathrm{li}=\Dmu_\mathrm{bulk}-\DGseq/\NA$, survives only as the named double-counting
demonstration of the contract (\texttt{Omega\_local\_ideal}, \texttt{Delta\_mu\_local\_ideal}).

\section{Homogeneous and heterogeneous nucleation}\label{th:sec:nucleation}

\textit{Physics.} A cluster of the new phase gains $\Dmu$ for every formula unit it contains but
pays $\gamma$ for every unit of interface it creates. Volume grows as $r^{3}$ and surface as
$r^{2}$, so small clusters are unstable and large ones grow; the barrier is the free energy at the
crossover. On a substrate the cluster can replace part of its interface with a cheaper
substrate contact, which lowers the barrier without changing the driving force.

\paragraph{Homogeneous barrier.} In the capillarity approximation, a spherical cluster of radius
$r$ with molecular volume $\vm=\Vm/\NA$ has
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dg-r}
\Delta G_\mathrm{hom}(r)=-\frac{4\pi r^{3}}{3}\frac{\Dmu}{\vm}+4\pi r^{2}\gnl \un{J}.
\end{equation}
Units: $r^{3}/\vm$ counts formula units, times J per formula unit; $r^{2}\gnl$ is m$^2$ times
J\,m$^{-2}$. Setting $\partial\Delta G_\mathrm{hom}/\partial r=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:hom}
r^{*}=\frac{2\gnl\vm}{\Dmu} \un{m},\qquad
\DGhom=\frac{16\pi\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{3\Dmu^{2}}=\frac{4\pi r^{*2}\gnl}{3} \un{J}.
\end{equation}
Units: J\,m$^{-2}\cdot$m$^{3}$/J $=$ m for $r^{*}$; J$^{3}$\,m$^{-6}\cdot$m$^{6}$/J$^{2}$ $=$ J for
$\DGhom$. With $\Dmu=\kB T\ln\Om$, $\DGhom\to\infty$ as $\Om\to1^{+}$ (no driving force, no
nucleation) and $\DGhom\to0$ as $\Om\to\infty$; it increases with $\gnl$ and $\Vm$ and decreases
with $T$ at fixed $\Om$. The absolute barrier in units of $\kB T$ is $\DGhom/\kB T$.

\paragraph{Spherical-cap geometry.} On a flat substrate the cluster is a cap cut from a sphere of
radius $r$, meeting the substrate at contact angle $\theta$ measured through the cap. Elementary
geometry gives base radius $\rho=r\sin\theta$, height $h=r(1-\cos\theta)$, and
\begin{align}
A_\mathrm{cap}&=2\pi r^{2}(1-\cos\theta),\qquad A_\mathrm{base}=\pi r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta \un{m^{2}},\label{th:eq:cap-areas}\\
V_\mathrm{cap}&=\frac{\pi r^{3}}{3}(1-\cos\theta)^{2}(2+\cos\theta) \un{m^{3}}.\label{th:eq:cap-volume}
\end{align}
The cap's free energy has three parts: the volume term, the free nucleus--liquid surface, and the
base, where substrate--liquid interface ($\gsl$) is replaced by substrate--nucleus interface
($\gsn$):
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dg-cap}
\Delta G_\mathrm{het}(r)=-V_\mathrm{cap}\frac{\Dmu}{\vm}+\gnl A_\mathrm{cap}+(\gsn-\gsl)A_\mathrm{base} \un{J}.
\end{equation}
Stationarity of Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dg-cap} with respect to $\theta$ at fixed cap volume is the
contact-line balance, Young's relation $\gsl-\gsn=\gnl\cos\theta$ (Section~\ref{th:sec:young}).
Substituting it, the surface terms become
\begin{equation}
\gnl\pi r^{2}\big[2(1-\cos\theta)-\cos\theta\sin^{2}\theta\big]
=\gnl\pi r^{2}(1-\cos\theta)^{2}(2+\cos\theta)=4\pi r^{2}\gnl\,f(\theta) \un{J},
\end{equation}
using $2-\cos\theta-\cos^{2}\theta=(1-\cos\theta)(2+\cos\theta)$, and the volume term is
$\frac{4\pi r^{3}}{3}f(\theta)$ times $-\Dmu/\vm$, with
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:f}
f(\theta)=\frac{(2+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}{4}=\frac{2-3\cos\theta+\cos^{3}\theta}{4} \un{1}.
\end{equation}
Hence $\Delta G_\mathrm{het}(r)=f(\theta)\,\Delta G_\mathrm{hom}(r)$ at every $r$: the cap has the
same critical radius as the sphere, and
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:het}
\DGhet=f(\theta)\,\DGhom \un{J}.
\end{equation}
The factorisation holds only because one and the same $\gnl$ multiplies $A_\mathrm{cap}$ and enters
Young's relation; this is the reason Section~\ref{th:sec:young} keeps the bare $\gnl$ in Young.

\paragraph{Properties of $f$.} $f(0)=0$ (complete wetting, no barrier), $f(\pi/2)=\tfrac12$
(hemisphere), $f(\pi)=1$ (no wetting, the homogeneous barrier). Its derivative is
$f'(\theta)=\tfrac34\sin^{3}\theta\ge0$ on $[0,\pi]$, so $f$ is monotone and
$0\le\DGhet\le\DGhom$ for every admissible $\theta$. $f$ is also the volume fraction of the cap
relative to the full sphere of the same radius.

\paragraph{Assumptions.} Capillarity (macroscopic interfacial energies at nucleus size), an
isotropic $\gnl$ (closer to the amorphous framing than to faceted calcite), a flat, homogeneous
substrate, no line tension, and a cluster of the bulk phase's composition. A non-classical
pathway (prenucleation clusters, liquid precursors) is outside this framework and is a
limitation, not a parameter.

\paragraph{Accessibility of the barrier.} A barrier is low enough only relative to the number of
attempts the system makes in the time available. Nuclei form on the template at the rate per
unit area
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:rate}
J=J_0\,\e^{-\DGhet/\kB T} \un{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}},
\end{equation}
with $J_0$ the kinetic prefactor of heterogeneous nucleation per unit template area (attachment
frequency, Zeldovich factor and site density). Over the time $t_\mathrm{p}$ that the template
spends at the registered \Rs\ state (slurry contact and drying), the expected areal density of
nuclei is $N_\mathrm{nuc}=J\,t_\mathrm{p}$; along a path of registered states $k$ held for
$\Delta t_k$ it is $\sum_kJ_0\Delta t_k\,\e^{-\Delta G^{*}_{\mathrm{het},k}/\kB T}$. Requiring at least the areal
density $N_\mathrm{req}$ gives the absolute-barrier criterion
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:access}
N_\mathrm{nuc}\ge N_\mathrm{req}\iff \DGhet\le B_\mathrm{acc}=\kB T\ln\frac{J_0\,t_\mathrm{p}}{N_\mathrm{req}} \un{J}.
\end{equation}
Units: $J_0t_\mathrm{p}$ and $N_\mathrm{req}$ are both m$^{-2}$, so the logarithm is of a pure
number. Equivalently, a count $\mathcal N$ on a collector area $A$ is $N_\mathrm{req}=\mathcal N/A$.
P0 registers $N_\mathrm{req}=1/A_\mathrm{patch}$, with $A_\mathrm{patch}$ the contact patch through
which a cathode particle touches the collector: at least one nucleus, in expectation, under every
patch. It is fixed separately from the closure density of C4 (Section~\ref{th:sec:conformality}),
so that C3 does not borrow the island geometry of C4.
Four properties make the criterion usable without a precise kinetic theory: only $\ln J_0$
enters, so an order-of-magnitude prefactor moves $B_\mathrm{acc}$ by a few $\kB T$; the barrier
is evaluated at $\Omb$ (Section~\ref{th:sec:template}); the non-stationary time lag is neglected;
and $N_\mathrm{nuc}$ ignores exclusion zones around growing islands, so it is an upper estimate
and the criterion is necessary, not sufficient. It is a statement about nucleation in the slurry,
not about corrosion, and no rate of anything else follows from it.

\section{Young's relation and the fixed sign convention}\label{th:sec:young}

\textit{Physics.} At the contact line, moving the line outward by a small amount replaces
substrate--liquid interface by substrate--nucleus interface and stretches the nucleus--liquid
surface. At equilibrium these balance. The contact angle therefore measures how much the
substrate prefers the nucleus to the liquid, in units of the nucleus--liquid energy.

\paragraph{Convention, in words.} $\theta$ is the angle at the three-phase line measured through
the nucleus. $\cos\theta$ is the free energy gained per unit contact area by replacing
substrate--liquid interface with substrate--nucleus interface, divided by the nucleus--liquid
interfacial free energy. $\theta=0$ means the nucleus wets the template completely; $\theta=\pi$
means it does not wet it at all.

\paragraph{Convention, as an equation.}
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:young}
\cos\theta=\frac{\gsl-\gsn}{\gnl},\qquad \gsn=\gsnz+\dgw \un{1;\ J\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
Units: a ratio of interfacial free energies. A finite angle exists only for
$|\gsl-\gsn|\le\gnl$. For $\gsl-\gsn\ge\gnl$ the nucleus phase spreads ($\Ssp\ge0$,
Section~\ref{th:sec:conformality}) and $f=0$; for $\gsl-\gsn\le-\gnl$ it does not wet and $f=1$.
An implementation clips $\cos\theta$ to $[-1,1]$ and reports which branch applied.

\paragraph{The water-displacement term.} The template is hydrated. When the nucleus forms on it,
the template-bound water layer under the contact disc must be displaced, and that costs free
energy over the contact area only. Define
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:dgw}
\dgw\equiv\gsn-\gsnz \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
where $\gsnz$ is a substrate--nucleus energy from a reference that does not include the removal
of that layer (for example, a contact computed without the template's structured water). The
sign convention is: \textbf{$\dgw>0$ is a cost.} From Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:young},
$\partial\cos\theta/\partial\dgw=-1/\gnl<0$, and with $\partial f/\partial\cos\theta=-\tfrac34\sin^{2}\theta\le0$,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:signflip}
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\dgw}=\frac{3\sin^{2}\theta}{4\gnl}\ge0 \un{m^{2}\,J^{-1}}:
\end{equation}
a positive displacement cost raises $\theta$, $f$ and $\DGhet$, and $\dgw<0$ lowers them. If
$\gsn$ is supplied directly as a full in-water interfacial free energy, the displacement is
already inside it, $\dgw\equiv0$, and the term must not be added again. Applying it twice is the
double-counting error that C3 item 13 demonstrates.

\paragraph{Why Young uses the bare $\gnl$ and not $\geff$.} The deck writes
$\geff=\gnl-\dgw$ and places it in the denominator of Young. That is rejected, for three reasons.
(1) Geometry: the displaced water sits on the contact disc. The free surface of the cap, whose
energy is $\gnl A_\mathrm{cap}$, never touches the template, so nothing about the template's
hydration changes $\gnl$. (2) Consistency: the factorisation of Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:het} requires the
$\gnl$ in Young to equal the $\gnl$ that multiplies $A_\mathrm{cap}$ and sets $\DGhom$. A modified
denominator would leave a residual surface term and break $\DGhet=f\DGhom$. (3) Sign: with
$\geff$ in the denominator,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:deck-sign}
\frac{\partial\cos\theta}{\partial\dgw}\bigg|_\mathrm{deck}=\frac{\gsl-\gsnz}{(\gnl-\dgw)^{2}} \un{m^{2}\,J^{-1}},
\end{equation}
which is positive on the wetting side ($\gsl>\gsnz$): a displacement \emph{cost} would lower the
barrier. This is design flag 3. $\geff$ is kept only as a named diagnostic, so that W4 can
reproduce the deck's form for the double-counting demonstration. The contract encodes the
reversal as sign-flip test SF1 (Table~\ref{th:tab:signflip}).

\section{Where the template acts: bookkeeping and the no-double-counting proof}\label{th:sec:template}

\textit{Physics.} The template is both an interface and a ligand field. In classical nucleation
the barrier depends on four quantities, $(\gsl,\gsn,\gnl,\Dmu)$; $\gnl$ belongs to the nucleus
and the solution, so the template can act only through $\gsl$, $\gsn$ and $\Dmu$.
Section~\ref{th:sec:supersaturation} showed that at equilibrium it cannot act through $\Dmu$. What
remains is to find where its Ca$^{2+}$ binding goes, to show that it is counted exactly once, and
to state the rule the screen follows.

\subsection{Two bookkeepings}\label{th:sec:bookkeeping}

\paragraph{Uniform chemical potential (U).} Every ion carries its bulk electrochemical potential,
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:uniform}; the driving force is $\kB T\ln\Omb$, Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dmu-uniform}; and
every interaction between the template and the ions near it is an excess quantity per area inside
the grand-canonical interfacial energies $\gsl$ and $\gsn$, evaluated at the bulk chemical
potentials.

\paragraph{Local-ideal (L).} The enriched Ca$^{2+}$ is given the bulk standard state at the local
concentration, so the enrichment raises the supersaturation, $\Omega_\mathrm{li}=(\cl/\cb)\Omb$, and
the interfacial energies are taken for the template without its Ca$^{2+}$ excess. This was
contract v1.

\paragraph{Which is consistent.} (U) is the equilibrium statement. (L) makes a hidden
assumption: an ion in the layer carries $\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca,bulk}-\DGseq/\NA$, more than the bulk
ion when $\DGseq<0$. That is the electrochemical potential of a layer that is \emph{not} in
equilibrium with the slurry, a separate reservoir held above the bulk. (L) is therefore
self-contradictory: $\Eenr$ is computed from the equilibrium condition~\eqref{th:eq:uniform} and
then used as if that condition did not hold. The binding free energy is spent once to hold the ion
at the site and credited a second time as driving force.

\paragraph{When a local excess of driving force is real.} A genuine $\Dmu_\mathrm{local}>\Dmu_\mathrm{bulk}$
needs a non-equilibrium state with a time scale: (a) concentration during drying, when
evaporation or a drying front outruns diffusive exchange with the remaining liquid; (b) slow
exchange of the layer with the reservoir, for example a layer loaded with Ca$^{2+}$ at one
activity and then exposed to another, or Ca$^{2+}$ released by protonation of the sites when the
pH changes, which leaves the layer above the bulk chemical potential until diffusion relaxes it;
(c) local pH excursions that shift the carbonate speciation at the interface. Each needs
transport and kinetics with a stated time scale. They lie outside a thermodynamic screen, which
evaluates equilibrium states at registered inputs, and P0 makes no statement about them. What a
screen can do is evaluate $\Omb$ at each registered state along the drying path, because
evaporation does change the bulk activities; that is the bulk driving force at a later state, not
a local excess.

\subsection{Exact reference: the grand potential of a cap}

At fixed $T$ and fixed bulk chemical potentials the open system minimises its grand potential.
Forming a cap of radius $r$ on the template transfers $n=V_\mathrm{cap}/\vm$ formula units from the
reservoir, each at the bulk chemical potentials, creates the cap surface and replaces template--liquid
interface by template--nucleus interface over the base:
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:grand}
\Delta\Omega_\mathrm{G}(r)=-n\,\kB T\ln\Omb+\gnl A_\mathrm{cap}+(\gsn-\gsl)A_\mathrm{base} \un{J},
\end{equation}
with $\gsl,\gsn,\gnl$ the grand-canonical (excess grand potential per area) energies at the bulk
chemical potentials. Within capillarity this is exact. The reservoir term carries no template
dependence, and by Gibbs' construction every effect of the template on the ions around it is an
excess per area: there is no other place for it to sit. Equation~\eqref{th:eq:grand} has the form
of Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dg-cap}, so the factorisation of Section~\ref{th:sec:nucleation} applies with
$\Dmu=\kB T\ln\Omb$ and the in-situ $\gsl$.

\subsection{Gibbs adsorption: where the bound Ca$^{2+}$ goes}

\paragraph{Template--liquid interface.} At fixed $T$ the Gibbs adsorption equation is
$\dd\gsl=-\sum_i\Gamma_i\,\dd\mu_i$. The Ca$^{2+}$ binding computations use charge-neutral cells,
and the adsorbing component is taken to be Ca$^{2+}$ with its compensating charge, that is, a
neutral solute. This is an approximation, and Section~\ref{th:sec:supersaturation}(ii) does not
justify it. The cancellation there concerns the ion product: the factors $\e^{\mp2e\psi/\kB T}$ of
the two ions cancel in $\aCa\aCO$ and in the neutral solid. It says nothing about the double-layer
(electrocapillary) part of $\gsl$. Binding Ca$^{2+}$ to anionic sites changes the surface charge
density $\sigma$, and with it the free energy of the diffuse layer, which is part of $\gsl$; for an
electrified interface the Gibbs equation carries the additional Lippmann term $-\sigma\,\dd\psi_0$,
with $\psi_0$ the surface potential. Equation~\eqref{th:eq:gibbs} is the neutral-solute
(Gibbs--Langmuir) term only, and the neglected double-layer term is a stated limitation (below).
Raising the Ca$^{2+}$ activity from zero
(the Ca-free template, $\gslz$) to the regime value at fixed pH and ionic strength, with the
Langmuir excess of Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:excess} and $\dd\mu_\mathrm{Ca}=\Rg T\,\dd\ln c$ (dilute
bulk, fixed activity coefficient):
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:gibbs}
\dga\equiv\gsl-\gslz=-\int_0^{\cb}\GCa(c)\,\Rg T\frac{\dd c}{c}
=-\Rg T\Big[\Gs\ln(1+x)-\ds\cb\Big] \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
because $\int_0^{\cb}\Gs\frac{x(c)}{1+x(c)}\frac{\dd c}{c}=\Gs\ln(1+x)$ with $x(c)=\Eenr c/\cmax$
linear in $c$, and $\int_0^{\cb}\ds c\,\frac{\dd c}{c}=\ds\cb$. Units: $\Rg T\Gs$ is
J\,mol$^{-1}\cdot$mol\,m$^{-2}=$ J\,m$^{-2}$, and $\ds\cb$ is mol\,m$^{-2}$. In terms of the C2 inputs,
$x=\exp(-\DGseq/\Rg T)\,\cb\ds/\Gs$, so $\dga$ is a function of $\DGseq$, $\aCa$ (through $\cb$),
$\Gs$ and $\ds$ only. Its properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\partial\dga/\partial\DGseq=\Gs\Theta>0$: each site contributes its occupancy, so deeper
binding lowers $\gsl$;
\item $\partial\dga/\partial\mu_\mathrm{Ca}=-\GCa$: the Gibbs equation itself;
\item at strong binding ($x\gg1$), $\dga\to\Gs\DGseq+\Rg T\Gs\ln(\cmax/\cb)$ plus terms that
vanish: each site gives its binding free energy, less the free energy of concentrating the ion
from the bulk concentration to that of a filled site;
\item a template that repels Ca$^{2+}$ ($\DGseq\to+\infty$) has $\dga\to+\Rg T\ds\cb>0$: negative
adsorption raises $\gsl$;
\item with no specific interaction ($\DGseq=0$) $\dga=\Rg T\Gs[y-\ln(1+y)]$ with $y=\cb\ds/\Gs$,
a pure site-exclusion correction that vanishes as $y^{2}/2$ in the dilute limit.
\end{itemize}
If $\gsl$ is computed directly at the regime activities, the adsorption is already inside it and
$\dga\equiv0$, the same rule as for $\dgw$ (Section~\ref{th:sec:young}).

\paragraph{Limitation: the double-layer term.} Equation~\eqref{th:eq:gibbs} omits the change of
the diffuse-layer free energy when binding changes the surface charge. In the low-potential
(Debye--H\"uckel) limit of Gouy--Chapman theory, a surface of charge density $\sigma$ carries a
diffuse-layer free energy $\sigma^{2}/(2\varepsilon_\mathrm{r}\varepsilon_0\kappa_\mathrm{D})$ per
area, where $\kappa_\mathrm{D}^{-1}$ is the Debye length and $\kappa_\mathrm{D}^{2}\propto I$, the
ionic strength. Units: C$^{2}$\,m$^{-4}$ over F\,m$^{-1}\cdot$m$^{-1}$ is J\,m$^{-2}$. At a fixed
change of charge the term therefore falls as $I^{-1/2}$: ionic strength screens it, and it
vanishes when binding leaves the charge unchanged. P0 does not add it, and does not assign it a
sign: it is an unquantified correction to $\dga$, largest for a template whose Ca$^{2+}$ binding
changes its charge strongly at low ionic strength. A $\gsl$ computed in situ with explicit ions
contains it, which is one more reason to prefer an in-situ $\gsl$ when one exists.

\paragraph{Template--nucleus interface.} $\gsn$ is a grand-canonical excess in the same sense as
$\gsl$: the excess grand potential per area of the template--crystal contact at the regime
chemical potentials. Under the contact disc the solution is gone, but the contact need not hold
the stoichiometric crystal on one side and the bare template on the other. Ca$^{2+}$ can sit in
excess at the contact, for example as an ion bridge between a carboxylate and the crystal surface
(carboxylate--Ca--calcite). With the dividing surface at the template and the excess counted
relative to the stoichiometric nucleus, such a Ca$^{2+}$ excess $\GCasn$ is inside $\gsn$, and the
Gibbs equation applies to it as it does to $\gsl$. With $\GCasl\equiv\GCa$ of
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:excess} the template--liquid excess,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:gibbs-sn}
\frac{\partial\gsn}{\partial\mu_\mathrm{Ca}}=-\GCasn,\qquad
\frac{\partial(\gsl-\gsn)}{\partial\mu_\mathrm{Ca}}=-\big(\GCasl-\GCasn\big) \un{mol\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
Units: J\,m$^{-2}$ per J\,mol$^{-1}$ is mol\,m$^{-2}$, an areal excess. The second relation is the
result of this subsection. The Young numerator $\gsl-\gsn$, and with it the sign of the template's
effect on the barrier, responds to the Ca$^{2+}$ activity through the difference of the two
excesses. Raising $\aCa$ raises $\cos\theta$ only if the contact holds more Ca$^{2+}$ excess than
the template--liquid interface does. \textbf{Both excesses are interfacial; neither is in $\Om$.}

The screen treats the two excesses differently because its inputs differ. $\GCasl$ is built from
C2 (Langmuir, Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:excess}) and enters through $\dga$ when $\gsl$ is supplied
Ca-free. $\GCasn$ has no C2 counterpart: it is a property of the template--crystal contact, which
$\DGseq$ (transfer from water to a site with water still around it) does not measure. It is
carried by $\gsn$ as supplied, so $\gsn$ (or $\gsnz$ with $\dgw$) is taken from contact data at
the regime state, computed or fetched for the template--crystal contact at the regime activities
(the C3 inputs). The rule is a rule for the supplied inputs: $\DGseq$ and $\dga$ are never added to
$\gsn$. It does not claim $\GCasn=0$. A contact energy supplied at another Ca$^{2+}$ activity would
need the shift $-\int\GCasn\,\dd\mu_\mathrm{Ca}$, which requires a model of the contact excess that
the screen does not have; such an input is flagged as off-state, never corrected with $\DGseq$.

\paragraph{Consequence for the barrier (Q1).} Young's relation becomes
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:young-ads}
\cos\theta=\frac{\gslz+\dga-\gsnz-\dgw}{\gnl},\qquad
\frac{\partial\cos\theta}{\partial\DGseq}=\frac{\Gs\Theta}{\gnl}>0 \un{1;\ mol\,J^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
At fixed $\gslz$ and $\gsn$ (the contact energy as supplied at the regime state), stronger binding
lowers $\cos\theta$ and raises $\theta$, $f$ and $\DGhet$. Ions held at the template--liquid
interface must be stripped from the area the nucleus covers, which is a cost; the physics is that
of an adsorbed inhibitor. A Ca-binding template promotes nucleation only if $\gsl-\gsn$ stays
positive after the adsorption term, that is, if $\gsn$ falls by more than $\gsl$ does. By
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:gibbs-sn} the sign of the template's response to Ca$^{2+}$ is the sign of
$\GCasn-\GCasl$: the Ca$^{2+}$ excess at the contact less that at the template--liquid interface.
The first enters the screen through $\gsn$ as supplied, the second through $\dga$. This is the
possibility v1 flagged in Q1, now settled: bookkeeping (L) gave $\DGseq<0$ a barrier-lowering
effect it does not have (sign-flip test SF2).

\subsection{The no-double-counting proof}

\paragraph{Statement.} In the screen composite
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:composite}
\DGhet=f\Big(\theta\big[\gslz+\dga(\DGseq,\cb,\Gs,\ds),\ \gsnz,\ \dgw\big]\Big)\,
\frac{16\pi\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{3\big(\kB T\ln\Omb\big)^{2}} \un{J}
\end{equation}
every effect of the template on the barrier is counted exactly once.

\paragraph{Proof.} (1) \emph{Exactness.} Equation~\eqref{th:eq:grand} is the free energy of cap
formation in the open system within capillarity; Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:composite} is its stationary
value after the factorisation of Section~\ref{th:sec:nucleation}, with $\gsl=\gslz+\dga$.
(2) \emph{Channel separation.} $\Omb$ depends on bulk activities only, so
$\partial\Om/\partial(\text{any template input})=0$. All template dependence is in $\gsl$ and $\gsn$,
hence in $\theta$; $\gnl$, $\Vm$ and $T$ are template-independent and may appear in both factors.
(3) \emph{Single entry of the binding.} $\DGseq$, $\Gs$ and $\ds$ enter $\gsl$ through
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:gibbs} only, and only when $\gsl$ is built from $\gslz$. They do not enter
$\gsn$, which is supplied from contact data at the regime state with its own excess $\GCasn$ inside
it (template--nucleus interface, above), and they do not enter $\Om$
(Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:dmu-uniform}). The water-displacement correction enters $\gsn$ once, by the
convention of Section~\ref{th:sec:young}. (4) Hence each template-dependent input appears once,
in one interfacial energy, and only through $\theta$. This completes the proof.

\noindent The checker verifies the syntactic form of (2) and (3) on the contract: the template
inputs are partitioned into the $\theta$ channel, the $\Om$ channel holds bulk inputs only, and
$\DGseq$, $\Gs$, $\ds$ reach the composite only through $\dga$.

\paragraph{The two errors, demonstrated.} The contract keeps two wrong composites, named and never
used for a verdict (C3 item 13):
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{DG\_het\_local\_ideal} (contract v1): Ca-free $\gslz$ with $\Omega_\mathrm{li}$. The
binding is counted once, but in a channel that does not exist at equilibrium, and it lowers the
barrier where the exact result raises it.
\item \texttt{DG\_het\_double}: the in-situ $\gsl$ (Gibbs term inside) with $\Omega_\mathrm{li}$. The
same binding free energy is charged to $\gsl$ and credited to $\Dmu$.
\end{itemize}
Both exceed the exact driving force by $\kB T\ln(\cl/\cb)$ per formula unit, which is
$-\DGseq/\NA$ in the dilute limit. The barrier of either is smaller than that of the exact
$\Om$-factor by $\big[\ln\Omb/\ln\Omega_\mathrm{li}\big]^{2}$, and \texttt{DG\_het\_double} reduces to
the v1 composite without the Gibbs term and to the screen composite without the local credit
(both checked). Together with the double application of $\dgw$ of Section~\ref{th:sec:young},
these are the double-counting demonstrations.

\subsection{Rule for the screen}\label{th:sec:rule}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The driving force is $\Dmu=\kB T\ln\Omb$ at the regime activities of Ca$^{2+}$ and
CO$_3^{2-}$. Neither $\Eenr$, $\cl$ nor $\Omega_\mathrm{li}$ enters the barrier.
\item C2's $\DGseq$ maps into the barrier only through $\dga(\DGseq,\cb,\Gs,\ds)$ of
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:gibbs}, added once to the Ca-free $\gslz$. If $\gsl$ is supplied at the regime
activities, nothing is added.
\item $\gsn$ is the grand-canonical energy of the template--crystal contact at the regime chemical
potentials, taken from contact data at the regime state (plus $\dgw$ if it comes from a reference
without the displaced water). Any Ca$^{2+}$ excess at the contact is inside it,
$\partial\gsn/\partial\mu_\mathrm{Ca}=-\GCasn$. $\DGseq$ and $\dga$ are never added to $\gsn$. This
is a rule for the supplied input, not a claim that $\GCasn=0$.
\item Carbonate needs no correction: a mean potential cancels in $\Om$ and in $\Dmu$.
\item The same rule governs R-slurry stability: dissolution of CaCO$_3$ is decided by $\Omb$. An
evaluation at an ``enriched'' state is a non-equilibrium sensitivity, never an equilibrium result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Film conformality}\label{th:sec:conformality}

\textit{Physics.} A barrier must be continuous: one through-path lets electrolyte reach Al. A
low nucleation barrier says where nuclei form easily, not whether they spread into a film. Whether
the mineral spreads or beads on the template is a wetting balance, and whether beads merge before
they grow thick is a competition between how many islands form and how far each spreads.

\paragraph{Spreading coefficient.} The free-energy change per unit area when a thick layer of the
nucleus phase replaces the template--liquid interface is
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:spread}
\Ssp=\gsl-\gsn-\gnl=\gnl(\cos\theta-1) \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
the second form holding on the partial-wetting branch where Young has a solution. Units: a sum of
interfacial free energies. Since $\cos\theta\le1$, a finite contact angle always has $\Ssp\le0$.
The sign condition is:
$\Ssp\ge0$ means no finite angle exists, $\theta\to0$, $f\to0$, and the phase grows layer by
layer (Frank--van der Merwe); $\Ssp<0$ means $\theta>0$ and growth by islands (Volmer--Weber).
Island-then-layer growth (Stranski--Krastanov) needs a strain or misfit energy that this
isotropic framework does not contain. The registered $\dgw$ enters $\Ssp$ through $\gsn$, with
the same sign as in $\theta$: a displacement cost makes spreading harder.

\paragraph{Coalescence of islands.} With $\Ssp<0$, a film forms only when islands meet. Let the
islands be equal caps of contact angle $\theta$ on a square lattice of density $\Ns$ (spacing
$\ell=\Ns^{-1/2}$). A cap of base radius $\rho$ has height $h=\rho\tan(\theta/2)$. Neighbours touch
when $2\rho=\ell$, and the base discs then cover only $\pi/4$ of the area; the square cells are
covered when $\rho=\ell/\sqrt2$. The two geometric thicknesses are
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:hc}
\hc=\frac{\tan(\theta/2)}{2\sqrt{\Ns}},\qquad
h_\mathrm{cl}=\frac{\tan(\theta/2)}{\sqrt{2\Ns}}=\sqrt2\,\hc \un{m}.
\end{equation}
Units: $\Ns^{-1/2}$ is a length. Both vanish as $\theta\to0$ and diverge as $\theta\to\pi$. They
are geometric estimates that treat caps as growing self-similarly up to contact; they are
arguments, not measurements, and $\Ns$ comes from a nucleation-and-growth model (item 17), not
from thermodynamics. The kinetic counterpart, the thickness at which islands actually coalesce in a
kinetic Monte Carlo or rate-equation model of nucleation and lateral growth (charter C4,
candidate (c)), is a different quantity and carries its own name, $\hcoal$ (cp2 fix F9). $\hc$
keeps its geometric meaning throughout; P0 does not compute $\hcoal$.

\paragraph{What a contact angle does and does not imply about coverage.} A small $\theta$ means
thin islands and early closure for a given $\Ns$. It does not fix $\Ns$, which depends on the
nucleation rate relative to lateral growth; it says nothing about places where the template is
absent, where the bare-oxide angle applies; and it is silent on porosity, on dissolution and
reprecipitation of the amorphous phase, and on cracking when a hydrated film shrinks on drying.
A C3 band $f(\theta)\le\tfrac12$ admits $\theta$ up to $\pi/2$, which is island growth;
conformality is therefore a separate criterion (C4, design flag 2).

\paragraph{The three C4 candidates and what each contains (lane note N1).} (a) A small-angle bound
$\theta\le\theta_\mathrm{max}$ and (b) $\Ssp\ge-s$ (with $s\ge0$ a margin) carry the same
information. On the partial-wetting branch Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:spread} gives $\Ssp=\gnl(\cos\theta-1)$,
so
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:n1}
\Ssp\ge-s\iff\cos\theta\ge1-\frac{s}{\gnl}\iff\theta\le\theta_\mathrm{max}=\arccos\Big(1-\frac{s}{\gnl}\Big),
\end{equation}
because $\cos$ is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ and $\gnl>0$. Both test one quantity, the Young
numerator $\gsl-\gsn$ relative to $\gnl$; they differ only in strictness. $\Ssp\ge0$ ($s=0$,
$\theta_\mathrm{max}=0$) is the strict end, complete wetting; it is also the better-posed form,
because it is defined when Young has no solution ($\gsl-\gsn>\gnl$), where $\theta$ is not. A
finite $\theta_\mathrm{max}$ needs a physical scale, and the only one the theory supplies is the
coalescence condition: closure below $d_\mathrm{max}$ at island density $\Ns$ requires
$\tan(\theta/2)\le d_\mathrm{max}\sqrt{2\Ns}$, i.e. $\theta_\mathrm{max}=2\arctan\big(d_\mathrm{max}\sqrt{2\Ns}\big)$.
(c) The coalescence condition, here in its geometric form $h_\mathrm{cl}\le d_\mathrm{max}$ (the
charter's kinetic form uses $\hcoal$), equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:nclosure}
\Ns\ge N_\mathrm{s,cl}=\frac{\tan^{2}(\theta/2)}{2d_\mathrm{max}^{2}} \un{m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
brings in $\Ns$, set by nucleation relative to lateral growth, and $d_\mathrm{max}$, set by
electron transport (Section~\ref{th:sec:film}). Neither (a) nor (b) contains either quantity, so
(c) is the independent condition. C4 therefore registers one criterion from the (a)/(b) family
and uses (c) as the independent supporting condition (\texttt{theory/thresholds.md}).

\section{Thermodynamic stability}\label{th:sec:stability}

\textit{Physics.} A layer that is thermodynamically unstable in its own environment dissolves or
reacts. The environment differs between regimes, so stability is two questions: in the slurry,
whether CaCO$_3$ and the native oxide survive alkaline water at the regime pH and potential; in
the cell, whether the phases survive the Li reservoir at the collector's potential. Both are
screens: necessary, not sufficient, and blind to morphology and kinetics.

\paragraph{\Rs: aqueous Pourbaix.} Each species $\phi$ (solid or dissolved) is brought to a
common reference by exchanging O and H with water, protons and electrons at the regime pH and
potential. Per non-O, non-H atom,
\begin{multline}\label{th:eq:pbx}
\Psi_\phi=\frac{1}{n_\mathrm{M}}\Big[G_\phi-n_\mathrm{O}\mu_{\mathrm{H_2O}}-\Rg T\ln10\,(2n_\mathrm{O}-n_\mathrm{H})\,\mathrm{pH}\\
-(2n_\mathrm{O}-n_\mathrm{H}+Q)\Fc\ESHE\Big] \un{J\,mol^{-1}},\qquad \DGpbx=\Psi_\phi-\Psi_\mathrm{hull}\ge0.
\end{multline}
Units: $G_\phi$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$ are J\,mol$^{-1}$, $\Rg T\ln10\,\mathrm{pH}$ is J\,mol$^{-1}$,
and $\Fc\ESHE$ is C\,mol$^{-1}\cdot$V $=$ J\,mol$^{-1}$. Species with $2n_\mathrm{O}-n_\mathrm{H}+Q>0$
are favoured by raising $\ESHE$, and species with $2n_\mathrm{O}-n_\mathrm{H}>0$ by raising pH.
$\Psi_\mathrm{hull}$ is the lower envelope over all species and mixtures of the same metal
composition, and $\DGpbx=0$ means stable. \emph{What it can say:} whether the phase is the
equilibrium state at the chosen (pH, $\ESHE$) and dissolved-ion activities. \emph{What it cannot
say:} rates, passivation, local pH at the interface, morphology. Two traps: the amorphous framing
lies above the calcite hull by its offset, so ACC is never on the hull and the tolerance must be
registered (C5 open item). Adding the offset to the edge makes the ACC test equal to the
calcite-level test: $\Psi_\mathrm{ACC}=\Psi_\mathrm{calcite}+\Delta_\mathrm{ACC}/n_\mathrm{M}$
and the hull is unchanged, so the test asks whether CaCO$_3$ is stable as a phase at the regime
activities, up to the framing's metastability. Whether ACC itself dissolves is $\Om$ with the ACC
solubility product, which C3 already carries and C5 reports as a labelled sensitivity (cp2 fix
F6); and the dissolved-ion activities chosen for the aqueous species decide
whether solids appear stable at all, so the screen can be satisfied by construction unless it is
shown to fail where it must (C5 item 21, Q8).

\paragraph{\Rc: Li-open grand potential.} In the cell the solvent is not water and the reference
is Li$^+$/Li. Holding the collector at $\ELi$ fixes the Li chemical potential,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:muli}
\muLi=\muLiz-\Fc\ELi \un{J\,mol^{-1}},\qquad \Phi_\phi=G_\phi-n_\mathrm{Li}\muLi \un{J\,mol^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
For a reaction $\phi\to\sum_k\nu_k\psi_k+\Delta n\,\mathrm{Li}$ that releases $\Delta n$ Li to the
reservoir, Li conservation gives
$\Delta\Phi(\ELi)=\sum_k\nu_k\Phi_k-\Phi_\phi=\Delta G_\mathrm{r}-\Delta n\,\Fc\ELi$, with
$\Delta G_\mathrm{r}$ the reaction free energy against Li metal. The phase is stable against
that reaction while $\Delta\Phi>0$, so each reaction sets one limit,
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:window}
E^{*}=\frac{\Delta G_\mathrm{r}}{\Delta n\,\Fc} \un{V},\qquad
\begin{cases}\Delta n>0\ (\text{delithiation, oxidation}): & \ELi<E^{*}\ \to\ E_\mathrm{ox}=\min E^{*},\\
\Delta n<0\ (\text{lithiation, reduction}): & \ELi>E^{*}\ \to\ E_\mathrm{red}=\max E^{*}.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Units: J\,mol$^{-1}$ over C\,mol$^{-1}$ is V. \emph{What it can say:} whether a phase reacts with
Li at the collector's potential. \emph{What it cannot say:} oxidation that exchanges no Li. A
Li-free phase such as CaCO$_3$ has no reaction with $\Delta n>0$, so its Li-open $E_\mathrm{ox}$ is
unbounded: the construction declares it stable at any anodic potential by construction. Anodic
stability of CaCO$_3$ and of the Al phases needs oxidation reactions that release CO$_2$, O$_2$ or
dissolved Ca$^{2+}$ and Al$^{3+}$ into the electrolyte, which require the electrolyte species as
additional open components (Q9). Kinetics and electrolyte oxidation are outside both screens.

\paragraph{The oxidation sink is fixed by the electrolyte (Q9).} When the collector oxidises
CaCO$_3$, the carbonate gives up its electrons and leaves as CO$_2$ and O$_2$. The Ca$^{2+}$ must go
somewhere, and the cell offers only what the electrolyte contains: Li$^{+}$, the salt anion A$^{-}$
and the solvent. Electroneutrality pairs each released Ca$^{2+}$ with two A$^{-}$, either as the solid
salt CaA$_2$ or as solvated ions, and the electrons reach the Li$^{+}$/Li reference as Li plated from
the salt LiA. Referenced to Li$^{+}$/Li, the reactions are
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:sink}
\mathrm{CaCO_3}+2\,\mathrm{LiA}\to\mathrm{CaA_2}+\mathrm{CO_2}+\tfrac12\mathrm{O_2}+2\,\mathrm{Li},\qquad
\mathrm{Al_2O_3}+6\,\mathrm{LiA}\to2\,\mathrm{AlA_3}+\tfrac32\mathrm{O_2}+6\,\mathrm{Li},
\end{equation}
each with $\Delta n>0$ and a limit $E^{*}=\Delta G_\mathrm{r}/(\Delta n\,\Fc)$ from
Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:window}. The sink is therefore not a free choice: it is the anion of the
registered electrolyte (\texttt{charter/regimes.yaml}, \texttt{electrolyte\_composition}). The rule
for choosing among candidate salts, once \texttt{lit/params.yaml} lists the fluorine-free salts:
(1) only the anions of the registered salt or salts are admissible; (2) the oxidation limit is the
minimum over the admissible sinks and over the products each can form, because a phase is
unstable if any accessible reaction is downhill; (3) the true product is the lower in free energy of
the solid salt and the solvated ions, so a limit computed with the solid salt alone is an upper
bound on the true onset: a falsification computed with it stands, and a pass computed with it is
inconclusive, so that a pass counts only with the solvated sink; (4) a sink whose thermochemistry
is missing makes the limit \emph{undetermined}, which never counts as a pass; (5) where the
CO$_2$ (or O$_2$) reference is uncertain, the limit is evaluated under each registered reference
scheme: a pass must hold under the scheme with the lowest onset, a falsification under every
scheme, and anything else is inconclusive (cp2 fix F5). \emph{Limitation:} the electrolyte's own
anodic limit is a separate quantity. Above it the solvent and salt oxidise first, and it is not
assessed here, so a pass says nothing about the stability of the electrolyte.

\section{The insulating-film limitation}\label{th:sec:film}

\textit{Physics.} CaCO$_3$ is an electronic insulator. The film lies between the cathode coating and
the Al collector, and every electron of the cathode current must cross it. A film that is
continuous but too thick blocks the collector, so conformality cannot be bought with thickness.
Two transport limits bound the admissible thickness; the numbers come later, through macros.

\paragraph{Ohmic limit.} If electrons cross by bulk conduction with resistivity $\rhoe$, the
area-specific resistance and the bound set by an admissible $R_{A,\max}$ are
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:ohm}
R_A=\rhoe d \un{\Omega\,m^{2}},\qquad d\le d_\mathrm{max}^\mathrm{ohm}=\frac{R_{A,\max}}{\rhoe} \un{m}.
\end{equation}
Units: $\Omega$\,m times m is $\Omega$\,m$^{2}$; $\Omega$\,m$^{2}$ over $\Omega$\,m is m.

\paragraph{Tunnelling limit.} For a thin film the current is carried by direct tunnelling through
a barrier of height $\phiB$. In the WKB approximation for a rectangular barrier the transmission
decays as $\e^{-2\kappa d}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:tun}
j=j_0\,\e^{-2\kappa d} \un{A\,m^{-2}},\qquad \kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m_\mathrm{e}\phiB}}{\hbar} \un{m^{-1}},\qquad
d\le d_\mathrm{max}^\mathrm{tun}=\frac{\ln(j_0/\jreq)}{2\kappa} \un{m}.
\end{equation}
Units: $\sqrt{\mathrm{kg\cdot J}}/(\mathrm{J\,s})=\mathrm{m}^{-1}$; the logarithm is of a ratio of current
densities. The bound is positive only for $j_0>\jreq$. The prefactor $j_0$, the effective mass and
the barrier height are inputs with large uncertainty (Q10), and defects or hopping can only raise
the current, so Eq.~\eqref{th:eq:tun} is a conservative bound for a defect-free film.

\paragraph{Combined with conformality.} The film must close before it exceeds the admissible
thickness:
\begin{equation}\label{th:eq:combined}
h_\mathrm{cl}=\frac{\tan(\theta/2)}{\sqrt{2\Ns}}\le d_\mathrm{max}=\max\big(d_\mathrm{max}^\mathrm{ohm},\,d_\mathrm{max}^\mathrm{tun}\big) \un{m}.
\end{equation}
This is a stated limitation (C4 item 18), not a performance figure. It contains no statement
about cell resistance, rate or capacity.
