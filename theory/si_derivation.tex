This section derives the relations the screen evaluates; each part opens with the physical argument in one sentence. Notation is that of the symbol table in the parameter tables, and displayed
relations carry SI units in brackets. Two regimes are kept apart: the water-processed, alkaline
electrode slurry at room temperature (\Rs) and the non-aqueous, fluorine-free cell with the Al collector
at the cathode's potential (\Rc); a relation is used only in the regime whose reference states it
assumes. The links C1--C5 are: anchor the template (C1), bind Ca$^{2+}$ (C2), nucleate the mineral
(C3), form a continuous film (C4) and keep it stable (C5). Meeting the conditions below makes the
mechanism thermodynamically possible under the stated framing; it does not show that a barrier works, and
no rate, capacity or device statement follows.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Adhesion of the template in water (C1)}\label{thsi:sec:adhesion}

\textit{Physics.} In the slurry the template never meets a dry oxide.

The Dupr\'e work to separate a contact between the oxide (o) and the polymer (p) in water (w) is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:dupre}
\begin{gathered}
\Wwet=\gow+\gpw-\gop=\Wdry+2\gw-W_\mathrm{ow}-W_\mathrm{pw},\\
\Wdry=\go+\gp-\gop \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $W_\mathrm{ow}=\go+\gw-\gow$ and $W_\mathrm{pw}=\gp+\gw-\gpw$ are the works with which water
binds each surface. The bare oxide has $\Wwet=0$. The route that decides C1 is the potential of mean
force (PMF) of a template fragment in explicit water, $\Wwet=\Delta F_\mathrm{PMF}/A_\mathrm{c}$.

No PMF is available for the main run, so the C1 verdict is a gap and counts as inconclusive. Beside it
are reported a labelled analog (works of adhesion in water for catechol-bearing films on other oxides,
which cannot represent displacement by water, so its verdict is conditional on the analog) and the
Dupr\'e form with ignorance-bounded energies $\gow\in[0,\go+\gw]$ and $\gop\in[0,\go+\gp]$
(Section~\ref{thsi:sec:branches}), which admit both signs of $\Wwet$. Masses under these bounds are
properties of the prior, never a finding; the flip value $\gop^{*}=\gow+\gpw-m_W$ is reported instead.

\subsection{The driving force: uniform chemical potential}\label{thsi:sec:uniform}

\textit{Physics.} A nucleus is built from ions taken out of the solution.

With $\Ksp$ the solubility product of the framing (calcite and amorphous CaCO$_3$ are both run, each
as a whole chain with its own $\Ksp$, $\Vm$ and $\gnl$),
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:omega}
\Om=\frac{\aCa\,\aCO}{\Ksp} \un{1},\qquad \Dmu=\kB T\ln\Om \un{J},
\end{equation}
per CaCO$_3$ formula unit, with molal activities. Nucleation requires $\Om>1$. For a hydrated framing
the ion activity product carries $a_\mathrm{w}^{n}$; keeping $\aCa\aCO$ overstates $\Om$ by
$a_\mathrm{w}^{-n}$ at a drying state ($a_\mathrm{w}<1$), so drying states are optimistic, $a_\mathrm{w}$
is reported there, and the hydrated ratio is a labelled sensitivity.

At equilibrium with the slurry reservoir every ion has the same electrochemical potential everywhere, so
the Boltzmann enrichment of an ion near the template is a consequence of uniform potentials, not an
exception to them. The driving force for adding a formula unit at $\mathbf r$ is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:dmu-uniform}
\Dmu(\mathbf r)=\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca}(\mathbf r)+\tilde\mu_{\mathrm{CO_3}}(\mathbf r)-\mu_\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}=\kB T\ln\Omb \un{J},
\end{equation}
the same at a binding site, in the diffuse layer and in bulk. Specific binding lowers the standard part
of $\tilde\mu_\mathrm{Ca}$ by $-\DGseq/\NA$ and raises the concentration term by the same amount, and a
mean potential cancels because the solid is neutral. The nucleus at the template is therefore driven by
$\kB T\ln\Omb$ at the regime activities, and the template changes the barrier only through interfacial
energies. A local driving force above the bulk one needs a non-equilibrium state with a time scale
(drying faster than exchange, a local pH excursion); the screen makes no statement about such states.

\subsection{Carbonate speciation and the activity model}\label{thsi:sec:speciation}

\textit{Physics.} $\Om$ needs the activities of the free ions.

The thermodynamic constants (at $I=0$) tie the ion-pair, bicarbonate and CO$_2$ activities to
$y\equiv\aCO$ and $\aCa$; dividing each by its activity coefficient and summing gives the carbon and
calcium balances, with $A_\mathrm{c}$, $P_\mathrm{c}$, $D_\mathrm{c}$ collecting the constants and
coefficients and $m_{C_\mathrm{T}}$, $m_{\mathrm{Ca}_\mathrm{T}}$ the totals in units of the standard
molality. The quadratic in $y$ has exactly one positive root:
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:aco3}
\aCO=\frac{2m_{C_\mathrm{T}}D_\mathrm{c}}{B+\sqrt{B^{2}+4A_\mathrm{c}P_\mathrm{c}m_{C_\mathrm{T}}D_\mathrm{c}}},\qquad
\aCa=\frac{m_{\mathrm{Ca}_\mathrm{T}}}{D_\mathrm{c}+P_\mathrm{c}\aCO} \un{1},
\end{equation}
with $B=A_\mathrm{c}D_\mathrm{c}+P_\mathrm{c}(m_{\mathrm{Ca}_\mathrm{T}}-m_{C_\mathrm{T}})$. The free Ca$^{2+}$
concentration $\cb=\aCa\rho_\mathrm{w}m^{\circ}/\gamma_{\pm2}$ is the $\cb$ of the binding relations below.
The activity model is Davies; a draw with $I$ above the adopted validity bound is refused and counted,
never extrapolated. Neutral species take coefficient one, and $I$ is an input rather than recomputed from
the speciation; both are stated limitations.

\subsection{Ca$^{2+}$ binding at the template (C2, a descriptor)}\label{thsi:sec:binding}

\textit{Physics.} The template can raise the local Ca$^{2+}$ concentration only if it holds Ca$^{2+}$
more strongly than water does.

For an acid site L--H that binds only when deprotonated, the cycle (deprotonate at the regime pH, then
transfer Ca$^{2+}$ from bulk to the site) gives
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:cycle}
\begin{gathered}
\DGseq=\DGsite+\Rg T\ln\big(1+10^{\,\mathrm{p}K_\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{pH}}\big),\\
\DGsite\equiv-\Rg T\ln\Big[\frac{1}{\Vsite}\int_\mathrm{site}\e^{-w(\mathbf r)/\kB T}\dd^{3}r\Big] \un{J\,mol^{-1}},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $w$ the PMF of one ion relative to bulk and $\Vsite$ the binding-layer volume per site. In the
dilute limit with non-interacting sites the enrichment is $\Eenr=\cl/\cb=\exp(-\DGseq/\Rg T)$; it never
enters $\Om$. For $\Gs$ sites per area in a layer $\ds$ thick (Langmuir),
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:langmuir}
\begin{gathered}
x=\frac{\Eenr\cb}{\cmax},\quad \cmax=\frac{\Gs}{\ds},\quad \Theta=\frac{x}{1+x},\\
\cl=\cmax\Theta\le\min(\Eenr\cb,\cmax),\\
\GCa=\Gs\Theta-\ds\cb \un{1;\ mol\,m^{-2}},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\GCa$ is the Gibbs excess: the bound amount per area less what the layer volume holds at the bulk
concentration. C2 reports these quantities and does not gate; its premise, $\DGseq<0$, is reported as a
finding (it holds, fails or is undecided under the shared decision rule). Each site holds one ion, two
bound ions cannot be closer than $2r_\mathrm{Ca}$, and a template of mass density $\rho_\mathrm{PDA}$ holds
at most $\rho_\mathrm{PDA}/M_\mathrm{u}$ moles of coordinating groups per volume ($M_\mathrm{u}$ the least
template mass per mole of groups). Hence
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:gamma-bound}
\begin{gathered}
0<\Gs\le\min\Big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt3\,\NA r_\mathrm{Ca}^{2}},\ \frac{\rho_\mathrm{PDA}d_\mathrm{layer}}{M_\mathrm{u}}\Big),\\
\max\big(2r_\mathrm{Ca},\ \Gs\Vm\big)\le\ds\le d_\mathrm{layer} \un{mol\,m^{-2};\ m}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The layer must contain the ion, cannot hold Ca$^{2+}$ more densely than a solid Ca phase and lies inside
the template layer. $r_\mathrm{Ca}$ is the ionic radius at the lowest coordination number adopted, the
smallest admissible, so the bound holds at any higher coordination; while it is unsourced the
$2r_\mathrm{Ca}$ term is dropped, which keeps the bound valid.

\subsection{Where the bound Ca$^{2+}$ goes: the Gibbs--Langmuir term}\label{thsi:sec:gibbs}

\textit{Physics.} Since the driving force is the bulk one, the only places left for the template's
binding are the interfacial energies.

At fixed $T$, $\dd\gsl=-\sum_i\Gamma_i\dd\mu_i$. Taking the adsorbing component as Ca$^{2+}$ with its
compensating charge (a neutral solute) and raising the Ca$^{2+}$ activity from zero (the Ca-free
template, $\gslz$) to the regime value, with the excess of Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:langmuir} and
$\dd\mu_\mathrm{Ca}=\Rg T\dd\ln c$,
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:gibbs}
\begin{aligned}
\dga&\equiv\gsl-\gslz=-\int_0^{\cb}\GCa(c)\,\Rg T\frac{\dd c}{c}\\
&=-\Rg T\big[\Gs\ln(1+x)-\ds\cb\big] \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
because $x(c)$ is linear in $c$. Deeper binding lowers $\gsl$, and a repelling template has
$\dga\to\Rg T\ds\cb>0$. If $\gsl$ is computed at the regime activities, $\dga$ is already inside it and
is not added. The equation treats Ca$^{2+}$ as a neutral solute and omits the Lippmann term from a change
of surface charge, an unquantified correction of unassigned sign that an in-situ $\gsl$ with explicit
ions contains. $\gsn$ is a grand-canonical excess too, with $\partial\gsn/\partial\mu_\mathrm{Ca}=-\GCasn$ if
the contact exchanges Ca$^{2+}$ with the reservoir. The screen takes $\gsn$ from contact data at the
regime state and never adds $\DGseq$ or $\dga$ to it; this is a rule for the inputs, not a claim that
$\GCasn=0$.

\subsection{Nucleation on the template and the sign convention}\label{thsi:sec:nucleation}

\textit{Physics.} A cluster gains $\Dmu$ per formula unit but pays interfacial free energy for every
unit of area it creates; the barrier is the top of that trade-off.

A sphere of radius $r$ with molecular volume $\vm=\Vm/\NA$ has
$\Delta G_\mathrm{hom}(r)=-\tfrac{4\pi r^{3}}{3}\Dmu/\vm+4\pi r^{2}\gnl$, so
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:hom}
r^{*}=\frac{2\gnl\vm}{\Dmu} \un{m},\qquad \DGhom=\frac{16\pi\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{3\Dmu^{2}} \un{J}.
\end{equation}
A spherical cap of contact angle $\theta$ (measured through the nucleus) on a flat substrate has
$\Delta G_\mathrm{het}(r)=-V_\mathrm{cap}\Dmu/\vm+\gnl A_\mathrm{cap}+(\gsn-\gsl)A_\mathrm{base}$.
Stationarity in $\theta$ at fixed volume is Young's relation, and substituting it factorises the whole
free energy:
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:young}
\begin{gathered}
\cos\theta=\frac{\gsl-\gsn}{\gnl},\qquad \gsn=\gsnz+\dgw;\\
\DGhet=f(\theta)\DGhom,\qquad f(\theta)=\frac{(2+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}{4} \un{1;\ J}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
$f(0)=0$, $f(\pi/2)=\tfrac12$, $f(\pi)=1$ and $f'=\tfrac34\sin^{3}\theta\ge0$, so $0\le\DGhet\le\DGhom$.
The factorisation needs one and the same $\gnl$ on the cap surface and in Young, so the bare $\gnl$
stays in the denominator.

Displacing the template's bound water under the contact disc costs free energy over the contact only:
$\dgw\equiv\gsn-\gsnz>0$ is a cost, placed on the substrate side, and a cost raises the barrier
(writing $\geff=\gnl-\dgw$ in the denominator instead would let a cost lower it and break the
factorisation). If $\gsn$ is a full in-water energy, $\dgw\equiv0$. With $\gsl=\gslz+\dga$,
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:young-ads}
\begin{gathered}
\cos\theta=\frac{\gslz+\dga-\gsnz-\dgw}{\gnl},\\
\frac{\partial\cos\theta}{\partial\DGseq}=\frac{\Gs\Theta}{\gnl}>0 \un{1;\ mol\,J^{-1}}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
At fixed $\gslz$ and $\gsn$, stronger binding lowers $\cos\theta$ and raises the barrier, as for an
adsorbed inhibitor. Binding promotes nucleation only if the contact holds more Ca$^{2+}$ excess than the
template--liquid interface.

\subsection{No double counting}\label{thsi:sec:nodouble}

\textit{Physics.} The template is both an interface and a ligand field, so its binding could be counted
twice: once as a lower interfacial energy and once as a higher driving force.

Forming a cap of radius $r$ transfers $n=V_\mathrm{cap}/\vm$ formula units from the reservoir at the
bulk chemical potentials, creates the cap surface and replaces template--liquid by template--nucleus
interface over the base. The grand potential change is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:grand}
\Delta\Omega_\mathrm{G}(r)=-n\,\kB T\ln\Omb+\gnl A_\mathrm{cap}+(\gsn-\gsl)A_\mathrm{base} \un{J},
\end{equation}
with grand-canonical interfacial energies at the bulk chemical potentials. Within capillarity this is
exact; the reservoir term carries no template dependence, and by Gibbs' construction every effect of the
template on the ions near it is an excess per area. Its stationary value is the screen composite
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:composite}
\begin{aligned}
\DGhet={}&f\Big(\theta\big[\gslz+\dga(\DGseq,\cb,\Gs,\ds),\ \gsnz,\ \dgw\big]\Big)\\
&\times\frac{16\pi\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{3(\kB T\ln\Omb)^{2}} \un{J}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Statement.} In Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:composite} every effect of the template on the barrier is
counted exactly once.

\paragraph{Proof.} (1) \emph{Exactness.} Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:composite} is the stationary value of the
exact grand potential~\eqref{thsi:eq:grand} after the factorisation of
Section~\ref{thsi:sec:nucleation}, with $\gsl=\gslz+\dga$. (2) \emph{Channel separation.} $\Omb$ depends
on bulk activities only, so $\partial\Om/\partial(\text{any template input})=0$; all template
dependence is in $\gsl$ and $\gsn$, hence in $\theta$. $\gnl$, $\Vm$ and $T$ are template-independent
and may appear in both factors. (3) \emph{Single entry of the binding.} $\DGseq$, $\Gs$ and $\ds$ enter
$\gsl$ through Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:gibbs} only, and only when $\gsl$ is built from $\gslz$. They do not
enter $\gsn$, supplied from contact data with its own excess inside, and they do not enter $\Om$
(Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:dmu-uniform}). $\dgw$ enters $\gsn$ once. (4) Hence each template-dependent input
appears once, in one interfacial energy, and only through $\theta$. This completes the proof.

\paragraph{The two errors.} Treating the enriched layer as a reservoir held above the bulk,
$\Omega_\mathrm{li}=(\cl/\cb)\Omb$, counts the binding once in a channel that does not exist at
equilibrium, and twice when combined with the Ca-loaded $\gsl$; a second application of $\dgw$ is the third
double count. The implementation keeps these composites only as named demonstrations.

\subsection{Admissibility of the template--liquid energy}\label{thsi:sec:admissibility}

\providecommand{\Pads}{\Pi_\mathrm{ads}}
\textit{Physics.} An interfacial free energy is the work of making unit area of interface.

At every state,
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:adm}
\gsl=\gslz+\dga\ge0 \un{J\,m^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
It is necessary, not sufficient, and an ultra-low positive $\gsl$ is admitted. With $\gsn\ge0$ and
$\gnl>0$ it makes all three in-water energies non-negative. Its Ca-free case is $\gslz\ge0$, where
$\gslz$ must be the same interface without Ca$^{2+}$ at the regime temperature, pH and ionic strength.
An energy measured with Ca$^{2+}$ at $c_\mathrm{ref}$ needs only $\dga(\cb)-\dga(c_\mathrm{ref})$ added;
adding $\dga(\cb)$ in full double-counts. An energy measured in pure water or from probe liquids on the
dry film lacks the pH and ionic-strength terms, so the sourced value overstates the reference. The
adsorption term is a small-change expansion about the Ca-free interface; its validity ratio
$r_\mathrm{ads}=-\dga/\gslz$ is reported, and Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:adm} is $r_\mathrm{ads}\le1$.

The bounds on $\Gs$ and $\ds$ do not by themselves keep $\gsl$ above zero. The lowering $\Pads=-\dga$
is largest where the excess vanishes and stays below $-\Gs\DGseq$, independent of $\ds$ and of $\gslz$;
for a hydrophilic film this is a fraction of $\gslz$ per few $\kB T$ of binding, so some doses overshoot.

The joint prior of $(\gslz,\DGseq,\Gs,\ds)$ is therefore restricted to Eq.~\eqref{thsi:eq:adm} at every
state and design point. Refused draws are counted, and the retained mass is reported beside every verdict
that uses $\gsl$. A saturating adsorption term and clipping $\cos\theta$ at $-1$ are rejected, because
each would invent a boundary state. The refused draws are the strongest binders. Finally, $f\le\tfrac12$
exactly when $\gsn\le\gsl$, so a favourable template needs a crystal--template contact at least as
favourable as the template--water contact, under any prior.

\subsection{Wetting branches, the spreading coefficient and the ignorance prior on $\gsn$}\label{thsi:sec:branches}

\textit{Physics.} Whether the mineral spreads on the template or beads into islands is a wetting
balance.

Replacing template--liquid interface by a thick layer of mineral changes the free energy per area by
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:spread}
\Ssp=\gsl-\gsn-\gnl=\gnl(\cos\theta-1) \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
the second form on the branch where Young has a solution. $\Ssp\ge0$ is complete wetting (layer growth,
$f=0$); $\Ssp<0$ is island growth.

Separating the template--nucleus contact into two surfaces against vacuum costs
$\gp+\gamma_\mathrm{n}-\gsn$, which cannot be negative, and a negative interfacial energy between
immiscible solids would make the system create contact without limit, so $\gsn\ge0$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:gsn-bounds}
0\le\gsn\le G\equiv\gp+\gamma_\mathrm{n} \un{J\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
with $\gamma_\mathrm{n}$ the dry surface energy of the framing phase. The same two arguments give
$\gow\in[0,\go+\gw]$ and $\gop\in[0,\go+\gp]$ (Section~\ref{thsi:sec:adhesion}). The ignorance prior is
uniform on the admissible range. 

Young splits the $\gsn$ axis at $a=\gsl-\gnl$ (complete wetting below) and $b=\gsl+\gnl$ (non-wetting
above). For $\gsn$ uniform on $[0,G]$ and the other energies fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:branches}
\begin{gathered}
P_\mathrm{complete}=\mathrm{clip}\Big(\frac{\gsl-\gnl}{G}\Big),\qquad
P_\mathrm{non}=\mathrm{clip}\Big(1-\frac{\gsl+\gnl}{G}\Big),\\
P_\mathrm{partial}=1-P_\mathrm{complete}-P_\mathrm{non} \un{1},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with clip to $[0,1]$. The marginal masses are expectations over the priors of $\gsl$ and $\gnl$. They
are properties of the prior and are never reported as a probability of feasibility; flip values and
required precisions are reported instead.

Because $\gsn\ge0$, $\Ssp\le\gsl-\gnl$, so complete wetting needs $\gsl\ge\gnl$. A template is called
hydrophilic here when water wets it at least as favourably, per area, as water wets the mineral,
$\gsl\le\gnl$ (not the usual small contact angle in air). For such a template $\Ssp\le-\gsn<0$ for every
admissible $\gsn>0$: complete wetting is excluded whatever the template--nucleus contact is, and
$P_\mathrm{complete}=0$. Ca$^{2+}$ binding lowers $\gsl$ further, so the mechanism's own premise pushes
the same way. Whether a template meets the condition is a question for the inputs, so $P(\gsl>\gnl)$ is
reported at every state.

With $\Ssp<0$ a film forms only where islands meet. Equal caps of angle $\theta$ on a square lattice of
density $\Ns$ close the film below an admissible thickness $d_\mathrm{max}$ only if
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:closure}
\begin{gathered}
\Ns\ge N_\mathrm{s,cl}=\frac{\tan^{2}(\theta/2)}{2d_\mathrm{max}^{2}} \un{m^{-2}},\\
d_\mathrm{max}=\max\Big(\frac{R_{A,\max}}{\rhoe},\ \frac{\ln(j_0/\jreq)}{2\kappa}\Big),\quad \kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m_\mathrm{e}\phiB}}{\hbar} \un{m},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $d_\mathrm{max}$ is the larger of the ohmic and direct-tunnelling bounds for an insulating film
that must pass the collector current; it is a limitation, not a performance figure. The lattice is the
optimistic arrangement, and the island density is the upper estimate $\Nnuc$ of the next part. A
continuous film is admitted through either route: complete wetting, or closure below $d_\mathrm{max}$.

\subsection{The absolute nucleation criterion (C3)}\label{thsi:sec:access}

\textit{Physics.} A barrier is low enough only relative to the attempts the system makes in the time
available.

Nuclei form on the template at the rate per area $J=J_0\e^{-\DGhet/\kB T}$, with $J_0$ the kinetic
prefactor per area. Along the \Rs\ states $k$ of the screen, held for $\Delta t_k$, the expected areal
density is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:nnuc}
\Nnuc=\sum_kJ_0\Delta t_k\,\e^{-\Delta G^{*}_{\mathrm{het},k}/\kB T} \un{m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
an upper estimate, since it ignores exclusion zones around growing islands; a state with $\Omb\le1$
contributes nothing. If nuclei land independently, the number under one patch of area $\Apatch$ is
Poisson distributed with mean $\Nnuc\Apatch$, and the fraction of patches left empty is
$\e^{-\Nnuc\Apatch}$. Requiring that at most a fraction $q_0$ be empty,
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:nreq}
\e^{-\Nnuc\Apatch}\le q_0\iff\Nnuc\ge\Nreq=\frac{\ln(1/q_0)}{\Apatch} \un{m^{-2}},\qquad q_0=1-p,
\end{equation}
with $p$ the credible level of the decision rule ($p=\NumPcred$, swept from \NumPcredSweepLow\ to
\NumPcredSweepHigh), so the admissible empty fraction moves with it. At a single state the criterion is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:bacc}
\DGhet\le B_\mathrm{acc}=\kB T\ln\frac{J_0t_\mathrm{p}}{\Nreq}=\kB T\ln\frac{J_0t_\mathrm{p}\Apatch}{\ln(1/q_0)} \un{J}.
\end{equation}
Only $\ln J_0$ enters, so an order-of-magnitude prefactor moves $B_\mathrm{acc}$ by a few $\kB T$.
$\Nreq$ is fixed apart from the closure density of C4, so that C3 does not borrow the island geometry of
C4. The part of a particle touching a flat foil lies inside its projection, and the particle touches
through at least one asperity contact, so
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:apatch}
a_\mathrm{asp}\le\Apatch\le\frac{\pi d_\mathrm{p}^{2}}{4},\qquad
\frac{4\ln(1/q_0)}{\pi d_\mathrm{p}^{2}}\le\Nreq\le\frac{\ln(1/q_0)}{a_\mathrm{asp}} \un{m^{2};\ m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
with $d_\mathrm{p}$ the projected-area-equivalent particle diameter. The smaller patch is the strict
end. The bound spans many decades and only its logarithm enters, so the prior is loguniform. While
either end is unsourced the criterion is reported as a gap, and the flip values of $\Apatch$ (where the
good mass equals $p$ and $1-p$) are reported in its place. The wetting part of C3 asks whether the
template prefers the nucleus to the liquid: $f\le\tfrac12$, $\theta\le\pi/2$.

\subsection{Thermodynamic stability (C5)}\label{thsi:sec:stability}

\textit{Physics.} A layer that is unstable in its environment dissolves or reacts, and the two regimes
are different environments.

\paragraph{\Rs: dissolution is decided by $\Omb$.} Carbon is held in the C(IV) carbonate system; the
construction with carbon redox allowed is a labelled sensitivity. Dissolving the layer is
CaCO$_3$(s)$\to$Ca$^{2+}$+CO$_3^{2-}$ at the regime activities, with free energy
$\Rg T\ln\Ksp-\Rg T\ln(\aCa\aCO)=-\Rg T\ln\Omb$ per mole. The layer dissolves when $\Omb<1$ and is
stable against dissolution when $\Omb\ge1$, with the measured calcite $\Ksp$ and the speciated
activities of Section~\ref{thsi:sec:speciation}. This is the same $\Omb$ that drives nucleation: a state
that cannot nucleate CaCO$_3$ is a state in which the layer dissolves, and enrichment at the template
changes neither. A computed Pourbaix hull distance cannot decide dissolution, because its tolerance hides several decades
of undersaturation; the test is applied at every design point and must report dissolution at an
undersaturated control state and stability at a supersaturated one, otherwise it is inconclusive. Whether
another solid lies below CaCO$_3$, within the tolerance of computed formation energies, is answered by the
hull distance $\DGpbx\ge0$ with C(IV) fixed and the dissolved Ca species removed. Adding the amorphous
offset to the edge makes the amorphous-framing test equal to the calcite-level test, because the offset
shifts the energy and leaves the hull unchanged. The native oxide's state is a baseline, not a gate.

\paragraph{\Rc: the window from explicit oxidation reactions.} Holding the collector at $\ELi$ fixes
$\muLi=\muLiz-\Fc\ELi$. A reaction that releases $\Delta n$ Li to the reservoir has
$\Delta\Phi=\Delta G_\mathrm{r}-\Delta n\Fc\ELi$ and sets the limit
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:window}
\begin{gathered}
E^{*}=\frac{\Delta G_\mathrm{r}}{\Delta n\Fc} \un{V};\\
E_\mathrm{ox}=\min_{\Delta n>0}E^{*},\qquad E_\mathrm{red}=\max_{\Delta n<0}E^{*}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
A Li-free phase has no Li-releasing reaction of its own, so a construction open in Li alone declares
CaCO$_3$ stable at any anodic potential by construction. Anodic stability needs reactions that hand the
carbonate's electrons to the reference and send the cation into what the electrolyte offers: Li$^{+}$,
the salt anion A$^{-}$ and the solvent. Electroneutrality pairs each released cation with A$^{-}$, and
the electrons plate Li from LiA:
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:sink}
\mathrm{CaCO_3}+2\,\mathrm{LiA}\to\mathrm{CaA_2}+\mathrm{CO_2}+\tfrac12\mathrm{O_2}+2\,\mathrm{Li},
\end{equation}
and likewise for corundum (Al$_2$O$_3+6$ LiA$\to2$ AlA$_3+\tfrac32$ O$_2+6$ Li) and boehmite (2 AlOOH
$+6$ LiA$\to2$ AlA$_3+$ H$_2$O $+\tfrac32$ O$_2+6$ Li), the three gated phases, each with $\Delta n>0$.

\paragraph{The sink.} The sink is set by the electrolyte of the screen, not chosen: only its anions are
admissible, and the oxidation limit is the minimum over admissible sinks and products, since a phase is
unstable if any accessible reaction is downhill. The true product is the lower in free energy of the
solid salt and the solvated ions, so a limit computed with the solid salt is an upper bound on the true
onset. A phase ruled out with it stays ruled out; a pass computed with it is inconclusive, and a pass
counts only with the solvated sink. A sink whose thermochemistry is missing makes the limit
undetermined, which never passes. The electrolyte's own anodic limit is a separate quantity that the
screen does not assess.

\paragraph{Margins.} Where every reactant and product is tabulated, the all-experimental route governs,
with margin $\mexpt$: the tabulated uncertainties per transferred electron plus a bound on any neglected
solid entropy (for the reaction with the perchlorate sink, $\mexpt=\NumMexpt$). A computed scheme
corrected by its measured benchmark error reproduces that route, so it checks the bookkeeping and is
never a second confirmation. Where the experimental route is missing, the computed schemes decide with
margin $m_E=n_\mathrm{unc}e_\mathrm{f}/(\Delta n\Fc)$, counting the atoms whose computed energy does not
cancel ($m_E=\NumMwindow$ for that reaction): a pass must hold under the lowest-onset scheme and a case
is ruled out only if every scheme rules it out. A margin belongs to one reaction and one sink. The rule is
\begin{equation}\label{thsi:eq:cell-rule}
\begin{gathered}
\text{ruled out if } E_\mathrm{ox}^\mathrm{expt}+\mexpt<\Ecmax\ \text{(either sink)};\\
\text{feasible only if } E_\mathrm{ox}^\mathrm{expt}-\mexpt\ge\Ecmax\\
\text{(solvated sink)},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
mirrored for reduction against $\Ecmin$. The charge cutoff $\Ecmax$ belongs to the cathode, so the
verdict is a function of it, with crossovers at $E_\mathrm{ox}^\mathrm{expt}\pm\mexpt$.
