\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

% (1) The problem: the aluminium collector in fluorine-free, water-processed electrodes, and its two stages.
Water-processed lithium-ion cathodes replace the fluorinated binder and organic solvent with fluorine-free binders cast from water\cite{heidbuchel2022enabling}. % CL-K01
Lithium leaching from the active particles then drives the slurry strongly alkaline\cite{hawley2020lithium,bauer2019effects}, and at that pH the native \ce{Al2O3}/\ce{AlOOH} film on the aluminium collector converts to hydroxide and dissolves, exposing the metal during slurry contact\cite{heidbuchel2022enabling,li2016effect,doberdo2014enabling}. % CL-K02, CL-K03
Anything that covers the collector therefore meets two sets of conditions: the alkaline aqueous contact between mixing and drying (the slurry stage) and the non-aqueous cell, where the collector sits at the anodic potential of the cathode (the cell stage). % CL-K04

% (2) The incumbents and the prior art, with the facts the prior-art scan recorded.
Two routes address the slurry stage today. % CL-V05, CL-V06
Phosphoric acid added to the slurry lowers its pH and so avoids corrosion of the aluminium collector\cite{chen2024study,carvalho2016study}, at the price of careful control of stoichiometry and drying against reactive phosphate residues\cite{tolchard2023insights}, and a carbon-coated collector keeps the slurry off the metal\cite{bichon2021performance}. % CL-V05, CL-V06
Phosphate films on the metal itself are claimed prior art: US~\NumPatentAlPhosA\ claims a film of \ce{Al_{x}M_{y}PO4} or \ce{Al_{x}M_{y}(PO3)3} on a metal plate of aluminium, copper, nickel or their alloys (example: a \NumPatentFoilUm\,$\mu$m aluminium foil), and US~\NumPatentAlPhosB\ claims the method, with a heat treatment between \NumPatentCureLow\ and \NumPatentCureHigh\,$^\circ$C\nacreMissing{patent citations, pending}. % CL-V03
The only test printed is immersion in hydrochloric acid for \NumPatentAcidHours\,h with no bubbles seen; the acid concentration is not stated. % CL-V03
A collector coating of a vitreous alkali-metal phosphate or polyphosphate with a conduction additive is claimed in US~\NumPatentLiPolyA, and US~\NumPatentLiPolyB\ claims the borate or polyborate form\nacreMissing{patent citations, pending}; the printed cell is a polymer-electrolyte cell cycled at \NumPatentCellTemp\,$^\circ$C with a recharge limit of \NumPatentRechargeV\,V. % CL-V04
On steel, films grow in situ from dosed polyphosphate and polyvalent cations, while the polyphosphate hydrolyses to orthophosphate in competition\cite{morsch2018unexpected}. % CL-V10

% (3) The biomineral lineage: what "nacre-inspired" and "bone-inspired" mean here.
Two biominerals that grow from water at room temperature name this paper's lineage. % CL-K12
Nacre is aragonite platelets in an organic matrix of proteins and chitin\cite{sedira2026nacre,osunamascaro2015ultrastructure}, grown through amorphous calcium carbonate precursors observed at its growth front\cite{devol2015nanoscale,gong2012phase}; chitosan, the partly deacetylated derivative of chitin\cite{younes2015chitin}, is the processable form used here. % CL-K12, CL-K06
In zebrafish fin bone, mineral arrives as amorphous calcium phosphate that transforms into apatite in the collagen matrix\cite{mahamid2010mapping}. % CL-K13
Here ``nacre-inspired'' and ``bone-inspired'' name this lineage only, an amorphous precursor that transforms under an organic matrix (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}). % CL-K14
The screen does not test layered architecture or polymorph structure, and alternating aragonite and calcite layers are not nacre-like. % CL-L10

% carbonate control (frozen)
% (4) The carbonate control, in one paragraph.
Carbonate, the mineral of nacre, was screened before the phosphates, and it runs through the same pipeline as their control: positive for the solubility condition in the slurry and negative for the oxidation condition in the cell (Section~\ref{sec:res-carbonate}). % CL-S02
In the cell, oxidation releases its calcium and lattice oxygen, and calcium carbonate is thermodynamically excluded\footnote{\label{fn:excluded}\NumExcludedNote.} above about \NumCarbonateCrossover\ for a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state); lithium carbonate, a different phase, oxidises close to the equilibrium value of its own reaction\cite{mahne2018electrochemical,cao2022oxidative}. % CL-S02, CL-R13
Aragonite, the polymorph of nacre, is not reported to form from solution at slurry pH and room temperature; it needs magnesium, a polyamine or heating\cite{nahi2022polyamines,hanifa2025effects}. % CL-K15
Calcite scale on aluminium alloys was porous with attack beneath it\cite{osoriocelestino2020influence}, and a polydopamine coating reduced carbonate deposition\cite{yu2023study}. % CL-E01, CL-E02

% (5) The regime-matched frame and the question.
The two stages ask different things of a mineral, so one phase need not serve both: a barrier must stay undissolved in the alkaline slurry and unoxidised at the cell cutoff, which the cathode sets. % CL-D19, CL-D04
The question is therefore matched to the regime: for each stage and each cutoff class, which mineral phases are thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark, which are left open, and which conditions decide? % CL-D19
It is a thermodynamic question: a condition that fails for every plausible input ends the chain unless a measured service limit shows kinetic stabilisation ($K_1$, tier two). % CL-R08

% (6) Bone mineral sits near carbonate, so bone-inspired chemistry means the condensed and aluminium phosphates.
Bone mineral does not escape the carbonate limit: oxidising hydroxyapatite or a tricalcium phosphate, like carbonate, strips calcium from a lattice of isolated anions, and their onsets at the solid-salt sink lie at \NumOnsetBoneLow\ to \NumOnsetBoneHigh, beside that of carbonate (Section~\ref{sec:res-carbonate}). % CL-S01
The calcium orthophosphates therefore share the low-cutoff frame of carbonate, and ``bone-inspired chemistry'' in this paper means the condensed calcium phosphates, reached by the amorphous-precursor route of bone mineral, with the aluminium phosphates screened beside them as non-biogenic comparators; their anions are linked into chains or bound to aluminium, and none is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark\ at any cutoff class (Section~\ref{sec:res-condensed}). % CL-D02

% (7) What the paper does and does not do.
This paper is a thermodynamic screen. % CL-P03
It states four conditions and the stage at which each applies (Section~\ref{sec:design}), bounds every input by sourced data (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), reports per phase and cutoff class whether a phase is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark, not thermodynamically excluded (open; defined in Section~\ref{sec:res-regime}) or undetermined, with the precision that would decide it, and turns the outcome into conditional predictions (Sections~\ref{sec:results} and~\ref{sec:predictions}). % CL-P03
