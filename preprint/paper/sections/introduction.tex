\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

% (i) The problem: the aluminium collector in fluorine-free, water-processed cathodes, and its two stages.
Water-processed lithium-ion cathodes replace the fluorinated binder and organic solvent with fluorine-free binders cast from water\cite{heidbuchel2022enabling}. % CL-K01
Lithium leaching from the active particles then drives the slurry strongly alkaline\cite{hawley2020lithium,bauer2019effects}. % CL-K02
At that pH the native \ce{Al2O3}/\ce{AlOOH} film on the aluminium collector converts to hydroxide and dissolves, and the metal is exposed during slurry contact\cite{heidbuchel2022enabling,li2016effect}. % CL-K03
A coating on the collector therefore meets two sets of conditions. % CL-K04
One is the alkaline aqueous contact between mixing and drying, the processing stage. % CL-K04
The other is the non-aqueous cell, where the collector sits at the oxidising potential of the cathode, the cell stage. % CL-K04
The two stages ask different things of a mineral, so one phase need not serve both. % CL-D19

% (i, continued) The incumbents and the prior art.
Two routes address the processing stage today. % CL-V05, CL-V06
Phosphoric acid added to the slurry lowers its pH and so avoids corrosion of the collector\cite{chen2024study,carvalho2016study}. % CL-V05
It needs careful control of stoichiometry and drying against reactive phosphate residues\cite{tolchard2023insights}. % CL-V05
A carbon-coated collector keeps the slurry off the metal\cite{bichon2021performance}. % CL-V06
On galvanised steel, a film grows in situ at a cut edge from what a strontium aluminium polyphosphate pigment leaches, after carbonate precipitates\cite{morsch2018unexpected}. % CL-V10
In that system the polyphosphate hydrolyses to orthophosphate in alkali\cite{morsch2018unexpected}. % CL-V10
Computed stability windows exist for lithium phosphate solid electrolytes\cite{binninger2020comparison,schwietert2021first}; the onset ladder here rests on tabulated thermochemistry and claims no priority. % CL-V10

% (ii) Biomineral chemistry as the source of candidate phases: what "nacre-inspired" and "bone-inspired" mean here.
Biominerals that grow from water at room temperature supply the candidate phases. % CL-K12
Nacre is aragonite platelets in an organic matrix of proteins and chitin\cite{sedira2026nacre,osunamascaro2015ultrastructure}. % CL-K12
It grows through amorphous calcium carbonate precursors observed at its growth front\cite{devol2015nanoscale,schmidt2024myriad}. % CL-K06
In zebrafish fin bone, mineral arrives as amorphous calcium phosphate that transforms into apatite in the collagen matrix\cite{mahamid2010mapping}. % CL-K13
Here ``nacre-inspired'' and ``bone-inspired'' name this lineage only, an amorphous precursor that transforms under an organic matrix (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}). % CL-K14
The screen does not test layered architecture or polymorph structure, and alternating aragonite and calcite layers are not nacre-like. % CL-L10
Aragonite, the polymorph of nacre, formed at no pH tested at room temperature in one study, only on heating\cite{hanifa2025effects}. % CL-K15
It is a minor product of additive-free solutions, generated by magnesium ions and polyamines\cite{nahi2022polyamines}. % CL-K15

% (ii, continued) What the screen tests: biomimetic, biopolymer-templated mineral coatings, and no binder.
The coatings in view are biomimetic, biopolymer-templated mineral coatings. % CL-L14
A catechol- or polydopamine-like template is meant to anchor to the oxide and to template the mineral. % CL-L14
Chitosan, the partly deacetylated derivative of chitin\cite{younes2015chitin}, is considered as a processable alternative template. % CL-K12, CL-L14
The screen tests the mineral phases and the thermodynamic stability of such coatings at both stages. % CL-L14
It does not test the mechanical or electrochemical behaviour of a binder, and a phase that the screen leaves open is a candidate, not a demonstrated coating. % CL-L14

% (iii) The open screening framework, the main contribution.
The main contribution is an open screening framework for such phases (Section~\ref{sec:framework}). % CL-P03
It takes sourced thermochemical data with their uncertainties, a list of candidate phases, and the potential and pH windows of both stages. % CL-P03
It tests each phase for dissolution at the processing stage and for oxidation at the cell stage. % CL-P03
It reports which phases are thermodynamically excluded\footnote{\label{fn:excluded}\NumExcludedNote.}, which stay open, and the precision of a measurement that would flip each verdict. % CL-P03
The test is thermodynamic: a condition that fails for every plausible input ends the chain, unless a measured service limit shows kinetic stabilisation. % CL-R08
The code and data are released with the paper, so a new phase can be screened by adding its data. % CL-P03

% carbonate control (frozen)
% (iv) What is applied here: the carbonate control, bone mineral beside it, and the condensed and aluminium phosphates.
Here the framework is applied to calcium carbonate and to calcium and aluminium phosphates. % CL-P03
Carbonate, the mineral of nacre, is the control: it passes the dissolution test in the slurry and fails the oxidation test in the cell (Section~\ref{sec:res-carbonate}). % CL-S02
For a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state), calcium carbonate is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark\ above about \NumCarbonateCrossover. % CL-S02
Lithium carbonate, a different phase, oxidises close to the equilibrium value of its own reaction\cite{mahne2018electrochemical,cao2022oxidative}. % CL-R13
Bone mineral sits beside carbonate: oxidising hydroxyapatite or a tricalcium phosphate also strips calcium from a lattice of isolated anions. % CL-S01
Their upper bounds at the solid-salt sink lie at \NumOnsetBoneLow\ to \NumOnsetBoneHigh, close to that of carbonate (Section~\ref{sec:res-carbonate}). % CL-S01
``Bone-inspired chemistry'' therefore means the condensed calcium phosphates in this paper, with the aluminium phosphates screened beside them as non-biogenic comparators. % CL-D02
The in-situ routes of Section~\ref{sec:routes} try to reach the condensed phases through an amorphous precursor, as bone mineral reaches apatite through amorphous calcium phosphate. % CL-D02
Their anions are linked into chains or bound to aluminium, and none is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark\ at any cutoff class (Section~\ref{sec:res-condensed}). % CL-D02
Calcite scale on aluminium alloys was porous with attack beneath it\cite{osoriocelestino2020influence}, and a polydopamine coating reduced carbonate deposition on carbon steel\cite{yu2023study}. % CL-E01, CL-E02

% (iv, continued) The question, the one narrow positive statement, and the scope sentences.
For each stage and cutoff class the screen asks which phases are thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark, which stay open, and which measurements would decide them. % CL-D19
The one positive statement is narrow: calcite is suitable against dissolution, a necessary condition only, over a named region of the processing grid (Section~\ref{sec:res-dissolution}). % CL-S17
