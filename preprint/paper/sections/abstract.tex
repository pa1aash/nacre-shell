\newcommand{\nacreAbstract}{%
We present an open thermodynamic screening framework for mineral coatings on aluminium current collectors in water-processed, fluorine-free lithium-ion cathodes, which meet an alkaline slurry during processing and an oxidising potential in the cell.
We apply it, with sourced thermochemistry and propagated uncertainty, to calcium carbonate and calcium and aluminium phosphates, minerals of the nacre and bone lineages and their condensed relatives, and report exclusions, not passes.
For a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state), calcium carbonate is thermodynamically excluded above about \NumCarbonateCrossover, and hydroxyapatite and the tricalcium phosphates (upper bounds \NumOnsetBoneLow\ to \NumOnsetBoneHigh) at \NumClassMidHigh\ and above.
The one positive statement is narrow: calcite is thermodynamically suitable against dissolution, a necessary condition, in the processing grid at pH \NumRegSlurryPhLow\ to \NumRegSlurryPhHigh\ and total calcium from \NumRegWindowFullLoCalcite\ (\NumRegSlurryPassFullCalcite\ cells).
Condensed calcium phosphates are not thermodynamically excluded, aluminium phosphates are undetermined, and no phase passes the oxidation test, because the transfer free energies that would give a lower bound are unmeasured.
The nucleation half of the model failed its retrodiction, and we state conditional predictions for one slurry-immersion and potential-hold experiment and the measurements that would decide each open verdict.%
