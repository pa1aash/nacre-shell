\newcommand{\nacreAbstract}{%
Aluminium current collectors in water-processed, fluorine-free lithium-ion cathodes meet an alkaline slurry during processing and an oxidising potential in the cell, and this thermodynamic screen tests mineral films of the nacre and bone lineages against both stages, per cutoff class.
The regime map gives a design rule: carbonate, the mineral of nacre, matches the alkaline processing stage and low cutoff voltages, while the condensed and aluminium phosphates of the bone lineage are the open candidates for high voltage.
At the solid-salt upper bound plus margin, for a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state), the oxidation ladder thermodynamically excludes calcium carbonate above about \NumCarbonateCrossover, hydroxyapatite and the tricalcium phosphates, with upper bounds at \NumOnsetBoneLow\ to \NumOnsetBoneHigh, at \NumClassMidHigh\ and above, and brushite in part; no phase passes, the lower bound needing unmeasured transfer free energies, and the aluminium phosphates are undetermined.
The one positive statement is narrow: the label reads thermodynamically suitable against dissolution (a necessary condition) for calcite in the processing grid at total calcium of at least \NumRegWindowFullLoCalcite\ (\NumRegSlurryPassFullCalcite\ cells, every state passing) and pH \NumRegSlurryPhLow\ to \NumRegSlurryPhHigh; it is not evidence of protection.
The nucleation half of the model is not validated.
If the screen's model holds, then in slurry immersion and anodic holds in a fluorine-free electrolyte a calcite film stays undissolved and unoxidised at low voltage, hydroxyapatite is thermodynamically excluded at the high hold, and condensed or aluminium phosphate films are not excluded there.%
