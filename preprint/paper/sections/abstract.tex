Aluminium current collectors in water-processed, fluorine-free lithium-ion cathodes meet an alkaline slurry during processing and an oxidising potential in the cell.
We screen calcium carbonate and calcium and aluminium phosphates, minerals of the nacre and bone lineages and their condensed relatives, against both stages with sourced thermochemistry and propagated uncertainty, and report exclusions, not passes.
For a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state), calcium carbonate is thermodynamically excluded above about \NumCarbonateCrossover, and hydroxyapatite and the tricalcium phosphates, with upper bounds of \NumOnsetBoneLow\ to \NumOnsetBoneHigh, at \NumClassMidHigh\ and above.
Condensed calcium phosphates are not thermodynamically excluded and aluminium phosphates are undetermined, and no phase passes the oxidation test, because the transfer free energies that would give a lower bound are unmeasured.
The one positive statement is narrow: calcite is thermodynamically suitable against dissolution in the processing grid (a necessary condition, not evidence of protection) at pH \NumRegSlurryPhLow\ to \NumRegSlurryPhHigh\ and total calcium from \NumRegWindowFullLoCalcite\ (\NumRegSlurryPassFullCalcite\ cells).
The nucleation half of the model failed its retrodiction and is not validated, and we state conditional predictions for one slurry-immersion and potential-hold experiment and the measurements that would decide each open verdict.%
