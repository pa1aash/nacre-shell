\section{Predictions}\label{sec:predictions}

% (1) The initial experiment.
The screen turns into one experiment that tests both stages with the same foils. % CL-Q01
Aluminium foil is tested bare, carbon-coated, and carrying a calcium carbonate film, a calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) film, a baked aluminophosphate film or a film made by one of the in-situ routes, in two tests: (a) immersion in a model alkaline slurry at the pH and calcium doses of the processing grid, and (b) anodic potential holds at \NumClassLo\ and at \NumHoldLow\ to \NumHoldHigh\ in a fluorine-free electrolyte, such as lithium perchlorate in a carbonate solvent, the salt of the solid-salt sink. % CL-Q01
The observables are the calcium, phosphorus and aluminium released into the immersion liquid or the electrolyte, the anodic current against the bare and carbon-coated foils, the gas evolved during the hold, and the composition of the film before and after each test. % CL-Q01

% (2) The conditional predictions, each with its observable and what would refute it.
If the screen's model holds, then a calcite film stays undissolved in the model slurry inside the passing region of Section~\ref{sec:res-dissolution} and dissolves at the low-dose corner, and it is not thermodynamically excluded at the \NumClassLo\ hold, and lithium carbonate oxidises only at \NumTierTwoLow; it is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark\ for a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state) above about \NumCarbonateCrossover, so at the higher hold it is expected to lose calcium with release of carbon dioxide. % CL-Q05
Calcium released inside the passing region would refute the dissolution reading; carbon dioxide at the low hold would refute the reading carried over from lithium carbonate, not the upper bound; a carbonate film unchanged at the higher hold would not refute the thermodynamic exclusion but would show kinetic stabilisation, a property the screen does not test. % CL-Q05
If the screen's model holds, then a hydroxyapatite film is not oxidised at the \NumClassLo\ hold and is thermodynamically excluded\nacreExcludedMark\ for a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated sink (solid-salt reference state) at the \NumHoldLow\ to \NumHoldHigh\ hold, above its crossover of \NumHapCrossover, where it is expected to lose calcium with oxygen evolution and leave pyrophosphate behind; calcium loss at the low hold would show an onset below the upper bound, which the ladder allows, and an unchanged film at the higher hold would again indicate kinetic stabilisation. % CL-Q02
If the screen's model holds, then a condensed calcium phosphate or aluminium phosphate film is not excluded at the \NumHoldLow\ to \NumHoldHigh\ hold: the ladder sets no bound below the hold for the calcium metaphosphate glass and none at all for the aluminium phosphates, so the hold measures what the screen leaves open. % CL-Q03
No outcome of a hold below an upper bound can refute it: oxidation below the bound of the calcium metaphosphate glass, \NumMetaGlassOnset, is allowed for every sink, so such a hold supports the bound or does not support it, and the ladder would be wrong only if a phase survived above its bound in a \ce{LiClO4}-saturated electrolyte, an outcome that would have to be told apart from kinetic stabilisation before it bore on the bound. % CL-Q03
The bound of \ce{Ca2P2O7}, \NumPyroOnset, lies inside the hold range, and for the aluminium phosphates the screen has no bound at all, so the hold measures a service limit where the screen has only a ceiling, and where it has none. % CL-Q03

% (3) What would decide the nucleation condition.
Whether any of these films can be grown on a template from the slurry is outside the experiment, and the screen states it only as a model statement. % CL-O04
A measured or computed template--nucleus energy of hydroxyapatite to within about \NumNucDecideWithin\ of zero would decide the nucleation condition, and a measured relative transfer free energy of \ce{Ca^2+} against \ce{Li+} into the cell solvent, to about \NumPrecTransferCaKj\ (a charge-balanced headroom), would decide the low class for the calcium orthophosphates and could admit the \ce{Ca(PO3)2} glass up to the highest class; nothing in this paper supplies either. % CL-O04
