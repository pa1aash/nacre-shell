\subsection{Templating thermodynamics}\label{sec:model}

% (1) The two regimes, and which links apply in each.
The mechanism is assembled in one environment and must survive in another. % CL-R09
The template is meant to anchor, bind \ce{Ca^2+} and template the mineral while the electrode is a water-processed, alkaline slurry at room temperature (\Rs). % CL-R09
The finished interface then sits in a non-aqueous, fluorine-free electrolyte, with the collector held at the anodic potential of the cathode (\Rc). % CL-R09
The two regimes differ in solvent, reference state and mode of attack, so each relation below is used only in the regime whose reference states it assumes. % CL-R09
Adhesion, \ce{Ca^2+} binding and nucleation exist only in \Rs. % CL-R09
Coverage is judged with the \Rs\ wetting energies against a thickness bound set by electron transport in \Rc. % CL-R09
Stability is the pair of conditions $K_2$ and $K_1$ of Section~\ref{sec:conditions} (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}). % CL-R09
The relations hold for any mineral phase, written per formula unit with its own solubility product, molar volume and nucleus--liquid energy. % CL-R09, CL-R05
The links are necessary conditions in series. % CL-R09
A link whose failing side carries at least the credible mass of the propagated inputs ends the chain. % CL-R09
A link that passes shows only that its step is thermodynamically possible. % CL-R09

% (2) Adhesion in water: the Dupre work with water competition.
In the slurry the template meets a hydrated oxide, so a template--oxide contact forms only by displacing water. % CL-R01
The work that decides it is the Dupr\'e work in water $\Wwet$, which already contains the displacement penalty (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}a; \nacreSIref{si:templating}{Templating detail}). % CL-R01
The bare oxide fixes $\Wwet=0$, and $\Wwet$ is a descriptor of the template, not a gate. % CL-R01, CL-D03

% (3) Ca2+ binding as a descriptor.
The template can hold \ce{Ca^2+} only if its coordinating groups compete successfully with the hydration shell of the ion. % CL-R02
The transfer free energy $\DGseq$ of one \ce{Ca^2+} from the hydrated bulk to a site measures that competition, and $\DGseq<0$ means that the ion accumulates at the sites (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}b). % CL-R02
Binding raises the local \ce{Ca^2+} concentration, but it cannot raise the driving force for nucleation. % CL-R02
Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}b therefore draws a few bound ions and the same sparse ion density at every height of the solution. % CL-R02
At equilibrium an ion has the same electrochemical potential at a site as in the bulk. % CL-R02
The driving force per formula unit at the template is therefore the bulk one, % CL-R02
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmu}
\Dmu=\kB T\ln\Omb,
\end{equation}
with $\Omb$ the bulk supersaturation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:stability}. % CL-R02
It is built from the free-ion activities $a_i$ at the slurry state, the ions $\nu_i$ and structural water $n$ per formula unit, and the solubility product $\Ksp$ of the framing. % CL-R02
Binding reaches the barrier only through the interfacial free energies. % CL-R02
Bound \ce{Ca^2+} is a surface excess of the template--liquid interface, and the Gibbs adsorption equation turns that excess into a change $\dga$ of the template--liquid energy. % CL-R02
The change is negative whenever the excess is positive (\nacreSIref{si:templating}{Templating detail}). % CL-R02
Treating the adsorbing component as \ce{Ca^2+} with its compensating charge, a neutral solute, is an approximation that leaves out the diffuse-layer correction (\nacreSIref{si:templating}{Templating detail}). % CL-R02
\ce{Ca^2+} held at the template--crystal contact is not part of $\dga$. % CL-R02
It belongs to the template--nucleus energy $\gsn$, which is taken from contact data at the slurry state (Fig.~\ref{fig:cycle}). % CL-R02
$\DGseq$ is therefore a binding descriptor, not a criterion of its own. % CL-R02, CL-D03
The consequence is plain: at a fixed template--nucleus energy, binding alone raises the nucleation barrier. % CL-R10
The bound ions must be stripped from the area a nucleus covers, as for an adsorbed inhibitor. % CL-R10
The template helps nucleation only if the template--nucleus energy falls by more than the adsorption lowers the template--liquid energy. % CL-R10

% (4) Heterogeneous nucleation: Young with the fixed sign convention, single entry, absolute barrier.
A cluster of the mineral gains $\Dmu$ for every formula unit it holds and pays an interfacial free energy for every area it creates. % CL-R03
Volume grows faster than surface, so the barrier sits at a critical size. % CL-R03
On the template a nucleus replaces template--liquid interface by template--nucleus interface over its base, which lowers the barrier without changing the driving force (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}c). % CL-R03
In the capillarity approximation for a spherical cap, % CL-R03
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het}
\begin{gathered}
\DGhet=f(\theta)\,\frac{16\pi}{3}\frac{\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{\Dmu^{2}},\qquad f(\theta)=\frac{(2+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}{4},\\
\cos\theta=\frac{\gslz+\dga-\gsn}{\gnl},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $\vm$ the volume per formula unit, $\gslz$ the template--liquid energy of the \ce{Ca}-free template, and $\Dmu$ the bulk driving force of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmu}. % CL-R03
The angle $\theta$ is measured through the nucleus: $\theta=0$ is complete wetting with no barrier, and $\theta=\pi$ is no wetting and the homogeneous barrier. % CL-R03
Every template-dependent input enters once, in one interfacial energy, and only through $\theta$. % CL-R05
$\Omb$ depends on bulk activities alone, while $\DGseq$, $\Gs$ and $\ds$ enter only through $\dga$, so no term is counted twice (proof in \nacreSIref{si:derivation}{Full derivation, No double counting}). % CL-R05
A barrier is low enough only relative to the number of attempts the system makes in the time available. % CL-R11
Nuclei form on the template at a rate per area $J=J_0\e^{-\DGhet/\kB T}$. % CL-R11
Over the time $t_\mathrm{p}$ that the template spends at the slurry states, the areal density of nuclei must reach a required density, % CL-R11
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access}
\DGhet\le B_\mathrm{acc}=\kB T\ln\frac{J_0t_\mathrm{p}}{N_\mathrm{req}},\qquad N_\mathrm{req}=\frac{\ln(1/q_0)}{A_\mathrm{patch}}.
\end{equation}
The required density follows from the contact patch $A_\mathrm{patch}$ and the fraction $q_0$ of patches allowed to stay empty, by Poisson statistics (\nacreSIref{si:templating}{Templating detail}). % CL-R11
Only $\ln J_0$ enters, so an order-of-magnitude prefactor moves $B_\mathrm{acc}$ by a few $\kB T$, and the criterion is necessary, not sufficient. % CL-R11

% (5) Film coverage: spreading, and why complete wetting is excluded when gamma_sl <= gamma_nl.
A low barrier says where nuclei form easily, not whether they make a continuous film. % CL-R12
A single gap leaves a path from the electrolyte to the collector. % CL-R12
Whether the mineral spreads over the template or beads up is set by the spreading coefficient % CL-R12
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spread}
\Ssp=\gsl-\gsn-\gnl\le\gsl-\gnl,
\end{equation}
where $\Ssp\ge0$ means complete wetting and growth layer by layer and $\Ssp<0$ means growth by islands. % CL-R12
Complete wetting therefore needs $\gsl\ge\gnl$. % CL-R12
A template with $\gsl\le\gnl$, an interfacial energy against water no larger than that of the mineral, cannot be wetted completely by a solid-like nucleus. % CL-R12
The screen reports the prior mass with $\gsl>\gnl$. % CL-R12
On such a template a continuous film can form only when islands meet before they grow too thick (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}d). % CL-R12
For equal caps on a square lattice, closure below an admissible thickness $d_\mathrm{max}$ needs an island density that rises steeply with $\theta$ and with $d_\mathrm{max}^{-2}$. % CL-R12
Electron transport through the insulating film in the cell sets $d_\mathrm{max}$. % CL-R12
A liquid precursor is the exception the route layer uses: its tension against the dilute liquid is small, so the bound no longer forbids spreading (Section~\ref{sec:routes}). % CL-R12, CL-D16

% (6) Stability: carried by the conditions.
Whether a film, once formed, persists is not a templating question. % CL-D05
Dissolution in the slurry and oxidation in the cell are the conditions $K_2$ and $K_1$ of Section~\ref{sec:conditions}. % CL-D05
A slurry state that cannot nucleate the mineral is one in which a film of it dissolves (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stability}). % CL-D05

% (7) What the model leaves out (stated briefly; the full list is in the Discussion).
The model leaves out four things, each taken up in the Discussion. % CL-L06
They are the validity of classical nucleation theory at nucleus size, the double-layer term of the adsorption energy, kinetics beyond the accessibility bound, and the persistence of the layer in the cell. % CL-L06
