\section{Physical model}\label{sec:model}

% (1) The two regimes, and which links apply in each.
The mechanism is assembled in one environment and must survive in another. % CL-R09
The template is anchored, binds \ce{Ca^2+} and templates the mineral while the electrode is a water-processed, alkaline slurry at room temperature (\Rs); the finished interface then sits in a non-aqueous, fluorine-free electrolyte with the collector held at the anodic potential of the cathode (\Rc). % CL-R09
The two regimes differ in solvent, reference state and mode of attack on the collector, so each relation below is used only in the regime whose reference states it assumes. % CL-R09
Adhesion, \ce{Ca^2+} binding and nucleation are defined only in \Rs, because water-wetted surfaces, the hydrated ion and an aqueous ion activity product do not exist in the cell; coverage is judged with the \Rs\ wetting energies against a thickness bound set by electron transport in \Rc; stability is a separate question in each regime (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}). % CL-R09
The links are necessary conditions in series: one that fails for every plausible input ends the chain, and one that passes shows only that its step is thermodynamically possible under the stated framing. % CL-R09, CL-R05

% (2) Adhesion in water: the Dupre work with water competition.
In the slurry the template never meets a dry oxide. % CL-R01
The hydroxylated \ce{Al2O3}/\ce{AlOOH} surface carries hydrogen-bonded water and the polymer is hydrated, so a catechol--oxide contact can form only by displacing that water (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}a). % CL-R01
The comparison that decides adhesion is therefore between the two states that compete in the slurry: oxide and template in contact, and oxide and template each wetted by water. % CL-R01
The reversible work per area that separates them is the Dupr\'e work in water, % CL-R01
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dupre}
\Wwet=\gow+\gpw-\gop=\Wdry+2\gw-W_\mathrm{ow}-W_\mathrm{pw},
\end{equation}
where the second form writes each in-water energy through the work $W_\mathrm{ow}$ or $W_\mathrm{pw}$ with which water binds that surface, and $\Wdry=\go+\gp-\gop$ is the adhesion against vacuum. % CL-R01
It states the water competition in one line: a large dry adhesion is offset by the binding of water to each surface, and a hydroxylated oxide and a hydrophilic polymer both bind water strongly, so $\Wwet$ can be small or negative where $\Wdry$ is large. % CL-R01
The dry work describes a state the slurry never presents and is kept only as a comparator. % CL-R01
The bare oxide has $\Wwet=0$ by construction and fixes the zero, and the template stays at the oxide against water if $\Wwet>0$. % CL-R01
$\Wwet$ is obtained as the potential of mean force of a template fragment pulled from the oxide in explicit water, divided by a contact area defined once for all fragments, which already contains the displacement penalty and the entropy of the released water. % CL-R01

% (3) Ca2+ binding as a descriptor.
The template can hold \ce{Ca^2+} only if its coordinating groups compete successfully with the hydration shell of the ion. % CL-R02
The transfer free energy $\DGseq$ of one \ce{Ca^2+} from the hydrated bulk at the regime activity to a site, built as a thermodynamic cycle that includes the deprotonation of the site at the regime pH and the activity corrections at the regime ionic strength, measures that competition, and $\DGseq<0$ means net binding (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}b). % CL-R02
Binding raises the local \ce{Ca^2+} concentration, but it cannot raise the driving force for nucleation. % CL-R02
The interfacial region exchanges ions with the slurry, so at equilibrium every ion carries the same electrochemical potential at a site as in the bulk: binding lowers the local standard potential of the ion by exactly as much as it raises the concentration term, and a mean electrostatic potential cancels in the neutral solid. % CL-R02
The driving force per formula unit at the template is therefore the bulk one, % CL-R02
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmu}
\Dmu=\kB T\ln\Omb,\qquad \Omb=\frac{\aCa\,\aCO}{\Ksp},
\end{equation}
with the free-ion activities of the carbonate speciation at the regime state and $\Ksp$ the solubility product of the framing. % CL-R02
Binding reaches the barrier only through the interfacial free energies. % CL-R02
Bound \ce{Ca^2+} is a surface excess $\GCa=\Gs\Theta-\ds\cb$ of the template--liquid interface, with $\Theta$ the Langmuir occupancy of sites of areal density $\Gs$ in a binding layer of thickness $\ds$ and $\cb$ the free-ion concentration, and the Gibbs adsorption equation turns that excess into a change of the template--liquid energy, % CL-R02
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gibbs}
\dga=\gsl-\gslz=-\Rg T\big[\Gs\ln(1+x)-\ds\cb\big],\qquad x=\frac{\ds\cb}{\Gs}\,\e^{-\DGseq/\Rg T},
\end{equation}
which is negative for a binding template; $\gslz$ is the energy of the \ce{Ca}-free template. % CL-R02
Treating the adsorbing component as \ce{Ca^2+} with its compensating charge, a neutral solute, is an approximation: it leaves out the change of the diffuse-layer free energy when binding changes the surface charge, a correction that ionic strength screens and to which the model assigns no sign. % CL-R02
\ce{Ca^2+} held at the template--crystal contact, for example bridging a carboxylate to the crystal, is not part of $\dga$: it belongs to the template--nucleus energy $\gsn$, which is taken from contact data at the regime state (Fig.~\ref{fig:cycle}). % CL-R02
$\DGseq$ is therefore a binding descriptor, reported with the occupancy, the excess and $\dga$, and not a criterion of its own. % CL-R02
The consequence is plain: at a fixed template--nucleus energy, binding alone raises the nucleation barrier, because the bound ions must be stripped from the area a nucleus covers, as for an adsorbed inhibitor. % CL-R10
The template helps nucleation only if the template--nucleus energy falls by more than the adsorption lowers the template--liquid energy. % CL-R10

% (4) Heterogeneous nucleation: Young with the fixed sign convention, single entry, absolute barrier.
A cluster of the mineral gains $\Dmu$ for every formula unit it holds and pays an interfacial free energy for every area it creates; volume grows faster than surface, so the barrier sits at a critical size. % CL-R03
On the template a nucleus replaces template--liquid interface by template--nucleus interface over its base, which lowers the barrier without changing the driving force (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}c). % CL-R03
In the capillarity approximation for a spherical cap, % CL-R03
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het}
\begin{gathered}
\DGhet=f(\theta)\,\frac{16\pi}{3}\frac{\gnl^{3}\vm^{2}}{\Dmu^{2}},\qquad f(\theta)=\frac{(2+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}{4},\\
\cos\theta=\frac{\gslz+\dga-\gsn}{\gnl},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $\vm$ the volume per formula unit and $\Dmu$ the bulk driving force of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmu}. % CL-R03
The sign convention is fixed: $\theta$ is measured through the nucleus, and $\cos\theta$ is the free energy gained per area by replacing template--liquid interface with template--nucleus interface, in units of the nucleus--liquid energy, so $\theta=0$ is complete wetting with no barrier and $\theta=\pi$ is no wetting and the homogeneous barrier. % CL-R03
Young's relation carries the bare $\gnl$, the same energy that sets the homogeneous barrier: water displaced from under the contact sits on the contact disc and never touches the free surface of the cap, and only one $\gnl$ lets the barrier factorise as written. % CL-R03
A displacement cost therefore enters $\gsn$ once, as a positive correction that raises $\theta$ and the barrier, and not at all when $\gsn$ is already a full in-water contact energy. % CL-R03, CL-R05
Every template-dependent input enters once, in one interfacial energy, and only through $\theta$: $\Omb$ depends on bulk activities alone, while $\DGseq$, $\Gs$ and $\ds$ enter only through $\dga$, added once to the \ce{Ca}-free $\gslz$, and not at all when $\gsl$ is computed at the regime activities. % CL-R05
Crediting the bound ions to the driving force as well would count the binding free energy twice and give it the opposite effect on the barrier; the proof that the composite of Eq.~\eqref{eq:het} counts each template input once is in the Supplementary Information. % CL-R05
A barrier is low enough only relative to the number of attempts the system makes in the time available. % CL-R11
Nuclei form on the template at a rate per area $J=J_0\e^{-\DGhet/\kB T}$, so over the time $t_\mathrm{p}$ the template spends at the registered \Rs\ states the areal density of nuclei must reach a required density, % CL-R11
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access}
\DGhet\le B_\mathrm{acc}=\kB T\ln\frac{J_0t_\mathrm{p}}{N_\mathrm{req}},\qquad N_\mathrm{req}=\frac{\ln(1/q_0)}{A_\mathrm{patch}}.
\end{equation}
The required density follows from the contact patch $A_\mathrm{patch}$ through which a cathode particle meets the collector: if nuclei land independently, the number under one patch is Poisson distributed, and allowing at most a fraction $q_0$ of the patches to stay empty gives the second relation. % CL-R11
Only $\ln J_0$ enters, so an order-of-magnitude prefactor moves $B_\mathrm{acc}$ by a few $\kB T$; the count ignores exclusion zones around growing islands, so it is an upper estimate and the criterion is necessary, not sufficient. % CL-R11

% (5) Film coverage: spreading, and why complete wetting is excluded when gamma_sl <= gamma_nl.
A low barrier says where nuclei form easily, not whether they make a continuous film, and a single gap leaves a path from the electrolyte to the collector. % CL-R12
Whether the mineral spreads over the template or beads up on it is a wetting balance, the spreading coefficient % CL-R12
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spread}
\Ssp=\gsl-\gsn-\gnl\le\gsl-\gnl,
\end{equation}
where $\Ssp\ge0$ means complete wetting and growth layer by layer and $\Ssp<0$ means growth by islands. % CL-R12
The bound holds because $\gsn\ge0$: a negative interfacial free energy between two immiscible solids would make them create contact without limit, so no sharp contact would exist. % CL-R12
It has a physical consequence: complete wetting needs $\gsl\ge\gnl$, a template--water interface that costs at least as much per area as the mineral--water interface. % CL-R12
A template that water wets at least as favourably as it wets the mineral, $\gsl\le\gnl$, therefore cannot be wetted completely by the mineral, whatever its contact with the nucleus, and \ce{Ca^2+} binding pushes the same way by lowering $\gsl$. % CL-R12
Hydrophilic is meant here in that relative sense: a small water contact angle in air constrains the energy of the template against water relative to its dry surface energy and does not by itself place $\gsl$ below $\gnl$, so whether a template meets the condition is a question for the inputs, and the screen reports the prior mass with $\gsl>\gnl$. % CL-R12
On such a template a continuous film can form only when islands meet before they grow too thick (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}d): for equal caps on a square lattice, closure below an admissible thickness $d_\mathrm{max}$ needs an island density that rises steeply with $\theta$ and with the inverse square of $d_\mathrm{max}$, so it is the nucleation density that must close the film. % CL-R12
$d_\mathrm{max}$ is set by electron transport through the insulating carbonate, a bound of the cell regime that enters only as a limitation. % CL-R12

% (6) Stability: R-slurry by Omega_bulk with Pourbaix as the competing-phase baseline; R-cell by carbonate oxidation.
A layer that is unstable in its own environment dissolves or reacts, and the two regimes pose different questions. % CL-R04
In the slurry, carbon stays in the carbonate system, and dissolving the layer is the reaction \ce{CaCO3(s) -> Ca^2+ + CO3^2-} at the regime activities, with free energy % CL-R04
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stability}
\begin{gathered}
\Delta G_\mathrm{diss}=-\Rg T\ln\Omb\quad\text{in \Rs},\\
E^{*}=\frac{\Delta G_\mathrm{r}}{\Delta n\,\Fc}\quad\text{in \Rc},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
per mole of \ce{CaCO3}, so the layer dissolves when $\Omb<1$ and is stable against dissolution when $\Omb\ge1$. % CL-R04
This is the quantity that drives nucleation: a slurry state that cannot nucleate the mineral is one in which a layer of it dissolves, and binding at the template changes neither. % CL-R04
Dissolution is therefore decided from $\Omb$ with the measured calcite solubility product, not from a computed distance to the aqueous stability hull, whose tolerance for the error of computed formation energies would hide several decades of undersaturation. % CL-R04
The aqueous Pourbaix construction at fixed carbonate carbon answers the remaining question, whether another solid lies below \ce{CaCO3} at the regime pH and potential, and gives the state of the native oxide as the baseline of the bare collector. % CL-R04
In the cell the solvent is not water and the reference is \ce{Li+}/Li: holding the collector at a potential fixes the chemical potential of lithium, and a reaction that releases $\Delta n$ lithium to that reservoir sets the limit $E^{*}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:stability}, above which the phase oxidises. % CL-R04
A carbonate exchanges no lithium by itself, so its anodic limit is the oxidation of the carbonate to \ce{CO2} and \ce{O2}; the released \ce{Ca^2+} must leave with what the electrolyte offers, which pairs it with two salt anions, while the electrons reach the reference as lithium plated from the salt, \ce{CaCO3 + 2LiA -> CaA2 + CO2 + 1/2O2 + 2Li}. % CL-R04
The sink is therefore fixed by the registered salt, and because the true product is the lower in free energy of the solid salt and the solvated ions, a limit computed with the solid salt alone is an upper bound on the onset: a falsification computed with it stands, and a pass needs the solvated sink. % CL-R04
The reaction free energy decides the limit, and its source matters: database solids combined with a gas reference carry the error of the carbonate formation energy and of the \ce{CO2} reference, and the registered reference schemes bracket the measured reaction energies from opposite sides. % CL-R04
Where every reactant and product is tabulated, the all-experimental route carries no such error and governs, and the computed schemes are benchmarked comparators. % CL-R04
The reading is physical: \ce{Li2CO3} in non-aqueous cells oxidises close to the experimental equilibrium limit of the same reaction\cite{mahne2018electrochemical,cao2022oxidative}, so the limit of \ce{CaCO3} marks the onset of an observable reaction rather than a bound that kinetics could postpone indefinitely. % CL-R13
The anodic limit of the electrolyte itself is a separate quantity and is not assessed. % CL-R04

% (7) What the model leaves out (stated briefly; the full list is in the Discussion).
The model leaves out four things, each taken up in the Discussion: the validity of classical nucleation theory at the size of a nucleus, with its capillarity approximation, isotropic $\gnl$ and lack of non-classical pathways such as prenucleation clusters or liquid precursors; the double-layer term of the adsorption energy; kinetics beyond the accessibility bound, including any local excess of driving force during drying, which needs transport with a time scale; and the persistence of the layer, since anchoring, binding and nucleation are not computed in the cell. % CL-L06
