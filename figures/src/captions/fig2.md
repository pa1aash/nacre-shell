## Draft

**Fig. 2** The mechanism chain the screen tests, link by link. Illustrative construction, not a simulated structure. In every panel the brown strip is the hydroxylated aluminium oxide (red discs, surface oxygens).

**(a)** Adhesion in water. On the left, interfacial water covers the bare oxide. On the right, catechol-bearing units (one 5,6-dihydroxyindole unit of the generic oligomer; nitrogen blue) bind to the oxide through their catechol oxygens. The water they displace leaves the contact (short arrows). Adhesion in water is the balance between this contact and the two water-wetted surfaces it replaces.

**(b)** Ca²⁺ capture. The catechol-bearing layer (beige band) carries catecholate and carboxylate sites, and a few of them hold a Ca²⁺ ion (green). The solution above holds the same sparse Ca²⁺ and carbonate ions at every height. Binding concentrates Ca²⁺ at the sites but does not raise the supersaturation, which stays that of the bulk.

**(c)** An amorphous CaCO₃ nucleus (pale blue cap) at a small contact angle on the template. The template–nucleus contact (vermillion) is where the template acts on the barrier. Ca²⁺ ions of the nucleus sit at the sites under the contact (ion bridging) as part of that contact.

**(d)** Coalescence of neighbouring nuclei into a continuous film over the template. The film covers the whole template–film contact (vermillion).

Pictograms are drawn from the structures' SMILES: bonds as lines; oxygen red, nitrogen blue, Ca²⁺ green, hydrogen white; carbon at line vertices; charges not drawn. Placeholders: dashed boxes, renders to follow.
