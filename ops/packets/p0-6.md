## d. The draft's main statements
**Abstract (copied from the built PDF):**

Aluminium current collectors in water-processed, fluorine-free lithium-ion cathodes meet an alkaline slurry during processing and an oxidising potential in the cell, and this thermodynamic screen tests mineral phases of the nacre and bone lineages against both stages, per cutoff class. The regime map gives a design rule: calcium carbonate, as calcite, meets the dissolution condition over part of the alkaline processing grid and is not thermodynamically excluded at the lowest cutoff class, and in acidadjusted slurries it is excluded against dissolution only below pH 6.5, while at high voltage only the condensed calcium phosphates, reached by the amorphous-precursor route of bone mineral, are not thermodynamically excluded and the aluminium phosphates, screened beside them as non-biogenic comparators, are undetermined. At the solid-salt upper bound plus margin, for a LiClO4 -saturated sink (solid-salt reference state), the oxidation ladder thermodynamically excludes calcium carbonate above about 4.02 V, hydroxyapatite and the tricalcium phosphates on their crystalline framing, with upper bounds at 3.98 V to 4.07 V, at 4.3 V and above, and brushite in part; no phase passes, the lower bound needing unmeasured transfer free energies, and the aluminium phosphates are undetermined. The one positive statement is narrow: the label reads thermodynamically suitable against dissolution (a necessary condition) for calcite in the processing grid at total calcium of at least 1 × 10−4 mol dm−3 (216 cells, every state passing) and pH 10.0 to 13.0; it is not evidence of protection. The nucleation half of the model is not validated. If the screen’s model holds, then in slurry immersion and anodic holds in a fluorine-free electrolyte a calcite film does not dissolve inside its passing region and is not thermodynamically excluded at the low hold, hydroxyapatite is thermodynamically excluded for a LiClO4 -saturated sink (solid-salt reference state) at the high hold, and condensed calcium phosphate films are not thermodynamically excluded there, while aluminium phosphate films are undetermined.

**Predictions (copied from the built PDF):**

The screen turns into one experiment that tests both stages with the same foils. Aluminium foil is tested bare, carbon-coated, and carrying a calcium carbonate film, a calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) film, a baked aluminophosphate film or a film made by one of the in-situ routes, in two tests: (a) immersion in a model alkaline slurry at the pH and calcium doses of the processing grid, and (b) anodic potential holds at 3.65 V and at 4.3 to 4.5 in a fluorine-free electrolyte, such as lithium perchlorate in a carbonate solvent, the salt of the solid-salt sink. The observables are the calcium, phosphorus and aluminium released into the immersion liquid or the electrolyte, the anodic current against the bare and carbon-coated foils, the gas evolved during the hold, and the composition of the film before and after each test. If the screen’s model holds, then a calcite film stays undissolved in the model slurry inside the passing region of Section 4.2 and dissolves at the low-dose corner, and it is not thermodynamically excluded at the 3.65 V hold, and lithium carbonate oxidises only at 3.8 V; it is thermodynamically excluded* for a LiClO4 -saturated sink (solid-salt reference state) above about 4.02 V, so at the higher hold it is expected to lose calcium with release of carbon dioxide. Calcium released inside the passing region would refute the dissolution reading; carbon dioxide at the low hold would refute the reading carried over from lithium carbonate, not the upper bound; a carbonate film unchanged at the higher hold would not refute the thermodynamic exclusion but would show kinetic stabilisation, a property the screen does not test. If the screen’s model holds, then a hydroxyapatite film is not oxidised at the 3.65 V hold and is thermodynamically excluded* for a LiClO4 -saturated sink (solid-salt reference state) at the 4.3 to 4.5 hold, above its crossover of 4.19 V, where it is expected to lose calcium with oxygen evolution and leave pyrophosphate behind; calcium loss at the low hold would show an onset below the upper bound, which the ladder allows, and an unchanged film at the higher hold would again indicate kinetic stabilisation. If the screen’s model holds, then a condensed calcium phosphate film is not thermodynamically excluded at the 4.3 to 4.5 hold and an aluminium phosphate film is undetermined there: the ladder sets no bound below the hold for the calcium metaphosphate glass and none at all for the aluminium phosphates, so the hold measures what the screen leaves open. No outcome of a hold below an upper bound can refute it: oxidation below the bound of the calcium metaphosphate glass, 4.89 V, is allowed for every sink, so such a hold supports the bound or does not support it, and the ladder would be wrong only if a phase survived above its bound in a LiClO4 -saturated electrolyte, an outcome that would have to be told apart from kinetic stabilisation before it bore on the bound. The bound of Ca2 P2 O7 , 4.45 V, lies inside the hold range, and for the aluminium phosphates the screen has no bound at all, so the hold measures a service limit where the screen has only a ceiling, and where it has none. Whether any of these films can be grown on a template from the slurry is outside the experiment, and the screen states it only as a model statement. A measured or computed template– nucleus energy of hydroxyapatite to within about 1.2 mJ m−2 of zero would decide the nucleation condition, and a measured relative transfer free energy of Ca2+ against Li+ into the cell solvent, to about 30 kJ mol−1 (a charge-balanced headroom), would decide the low class for the calcium orthophosphates and could admit the Ca(PO3 )2 glass up to the highest class; nothing in this paper supplies either.
