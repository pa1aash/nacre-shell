## Questions to expect, with draft answers
1. Why is the solid-perchlorate onset an upper bound? Draft: the solid salt is one product state, so the true onset is at most its onset. That holds for an electrolyte saturated in the salt; in an undersaturated one the bound rises by (2RT/F)ln(a_sat/a), now bounded (after B3).
2. Why not give E_lb? Draft: it is computed from aqueous data and printed; it bounds the onset only if Ca2+ loses at least twice the solvation of Li+ from water to PC, which no datum tests.
3. Why read Li2CO3 for CaCO3? Draft: it is a bulk record of another phase, close to its own equilibrium; it words "low voltage", decides nothing, and the contrary 3.5 V reading is in the SI.
4. Bare aluminium pits in LiClO4 near 4 V; what does the hold show? Draft: release of Ca, P and Al and the gas evolved, against bare and carbon-coated foil; no protection is claimed.
5. Does calcite form on alumina at pH 10 to 13? Draft: not shown; the label is necessary only; the nucleation half failed retrodiction and K3 is undetermined.
6. Why are aluminium phosphates "bone-inspired"? Draft: they are not; they are comparators beside the condensed calcium phosphates (M22).
7. Are grade-D inputs (alpha-TCP K_sp, ACP K_sp, the acid pH grid) decisive? Draft: grades are printed; verdicts that rest on them are marked; the precision map gives the flip values.
