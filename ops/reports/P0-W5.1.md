## R-slurry (A2)

**Thermodynamic questions.** (1) Which Al/Ca/C species combination is at equilibrium at each
(pH, E vs SHE)? (2) How far above that equilibrium do calcite, ACC, corundum, boehmite and diaspore
sit? (3) With crystalline CaCO3 excluded, where is ACC stable, and how does that region move
with the ACC offset? All three are equilibrium proxies, with no rate statement.

**Regime settings and their sources** (`results/preprint/pourbaix/config/mock.yaml`).

| Input | Value (mock run) | Source |
|---|---|---|

| pH min | 7 | charter `R-slurry.inputs.pH.range.min` (definitional) |
| pH max | 14 | mock; the charter upper bound is pending S12 |

| Carbonate system | closed | choice (below) |
| ACC offset | 10 kJ/mol per CaCO3 above calcite | mock, refused outside `--mock` |

**Carbonate system: closed.** The charter makes carbonate speciation an input fixed at the
regime pH from the carbonate source, and C3 uses a_CO3 as a stated input. A slurry cast and
dried in a mixer is not assumed to equilibrate with air. An open system would tie a_CO3 to
p(CO2) and make it rise a hundredfold per pH unit, turning an input into an output. Total
carbonate C_T is therefore fixed, and the C(IV) speciation follows from pH.

The acid constants come from the same cached ion energies the diagram uses: pKa1 = 6.383 and
pKa2 = 10.342 (derived, not typed). The diagram uses the Pourbaix predominance convention, with
each ion at the element total. This matches the closed-system speciation except within about one
pH unit of a pKa.

**ACC.** ACC enters as calcite (mp-3953, R-3c, the lowest-energy CaCO3 in MP) plus the offset.
The offset will be read from `lit/params.yaml` (`source: lit/params.yaml`, key from P0-W1.2).
W1 evidence rows that feed it, cited without values:

- TC-030 and TC-031 (ACC excess free energy);
- TC-037, TC-038, TC-039, TC-040, TC-041 and TC-114 (K_sp of ACC and its variability);
- TC-026, TC-027, TC-028 and TC-029 (ACC crystallisation enthalpy, calorimetry).

W3 Q13 (structural water in the IAP of ACC) bears on how the offset is defined. The offset is
per anhydrous CaCO3 formula unit here.

## R-cell window (A3)

**Thermodynamic question.** Between which potentials vs Li+/Li are CaCO3 and Al2O3 at
equilibrium against lithiation, and against oxidation to O2 (and CO2) with the cation going to a
stated sink? This is a thermodynamic window, not a kinetic statement.

**Convention.** This is W3's contract `E_window = Delta_G_r/(Delta_n_Li F_c)` at contract commit
`b5a944cd335eb0a8d2e708b4391c9ad82ad31dc3`. Delta_n_Li > 0 releases Li (oxidation), and E_ox is
the minimum over those reactions, while E_red is the maximum over lithiation reactions. The test
evaluates the contract expression with sympy from the git object and checks the code against it.
The sign convention matches W4's `reaction_limit_potential`.

**Reduction limits** (Li-open grand potential, 0 K, MP2020; first lithiation reaction on the hull):

| Phase | E_red (V vs Li+/Li) | Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| CaCO3 (mp-3953) | 1.623 | CaCO3 + 1.333 Li -> 0.6667 Li2CO3 + CaO + 0.3333 C |
| Al2O3 (mp-1143) | 1.221 | Al2O3 + 0.375 Li -> 0.375 LiAl5O8 + 0.125 Al |

The CaCO3 value is checked against an independent recomputation from product formation energies
(tested). Both phases sit on their 0 K hulls (e_above_hull = 0).

**Oxidation limits.** The Li-open construction returns an unbounded E_ox for both phases (Q9).
That value is replaced by explicit oxidation reactions, in which the anion's electrons are
referenced to Li/Li+ through Li plated from the electrolyte salt:

- **Solids.** MP formation energy, 0 K, entropy neglected.
- **O2.** dfH = 0, because the MP2020 oxide correction is fitted against the computed O2
  reference. S(T) comes from JANAF O-029.
- **CO2.** dfH and S(T) come from JANAF C-095 (scheme `janaf`). The sensitivity scheme `mp` uses
  the MP CO2 molecular solid, without sublimation.
- **Conditions.** T = 298.15 K and p = 1 bar, both mock.

Every candidate reaction, with its onset in V vs Li+/Li:

| Phase | Sink | Reaction | Onset, CO2 `janaf` | Onset, CO2 `mp` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CaCO3 | nitrate (active) | CaCO3 + 2 LiNO3 -> Ca(NO3)2 + 0.5 O2 + CO2 + 2 Li | **4.274** | 3.659 |
| CaCO3 | sulfate | CaCO3 + Li2SO4 -> CaSO4 + 0.5 O2 + CO2 + 2 Li | 4.275 | 3.660 |
| CaCO3 | chloride | CaCO3 + 2 LiCl -> CaCl2 + 0.5 O2 + CO2 + 2 Li | 4.261 | 3.646 |
| CaCO3 | solvated | Ca2+ in the electrolyte | undetermined: needs the Ca2+ vs Li+ solvation free energy | — |
| Al2O3 | nitrate (active) | Al2O3 + 6 LiNO3 -> 2 Al(NO3)3 + 1.5 O2 + 6 Li | **undetermined**: Al(NO3)3 is not in MP 2026.04.13 | — |
| Al2O3 | sulfate | Al2O3 + 3 Li2SO4 -> Al2(SO4)3 + 1.5 O2 + 6 Li | 4.171 | — |
| Al2O3 | chloride | Al2O3 + 6 LiCl -> 2 AlCl3 + 1.5 O2 + 6 Li | 4.350 | — |
| Al2O3 | solvated | Al3+ in the electrolyte | undetermined | — |

- **Conditional oxidation limits, active sink nitrate (mock).** CaCO3: 4.274 V. Al2O3:
  undetermined, because the sink phase is missing from the database.
- **Sink assumption, for the cp2 Q9 audit.** The cation leaves as the solid salt of the
  electrolyte anion. The anion comes from the Li salt, whose Li is plated at the Li+/Li
  reference. The sink is `regimes/R-cell/active_sink` in the run configuration, so the cp2
  ruling switches it with no code change (tested with sulfate). The electrolyte's own anodic
  stability (oxidation of the anion or solvent) is not assessed. It could set a lower practical
  limit than any value here.
- **CO2 reference sensitivity.** The CO2 enthalpy reference moves the CaCO3 oxidation onset by
  0.61 V. The chemical check CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2 is uphill under both schemes (+2.089 eV with
  `janaf`, +0.859 eV with `mp`), so CaCO3 is stable against CO2 loss either way. The spread,
  however, shows that mixing MP solid energies with an experimental CO2 enthalpy is the largest
  uncertainty in the CaCO3 oxidation limit. P0-W5.2 should benchmark this reaction against a
  fetched experimental value (a need from W1, below).
- **Guard.** No phase is ever reported with an unbounded window.
  - `guard()` refuses an infinite limit, or an undetermined one without a reason.
  - `build_window` turns the Li-open infinity into the explicit oxidation limit, or into
    `undetermined` ("no oxidation reaction supplied").
  - `screen_pass()` returns False for any undetermined limit.
  - The guard test on a Li-free toy phase with no oxidation reaction passes.
- **Method reference.** W1 row TE-047 (Zhu, He and Mo 2016, 10.1039/c5ta08574h, locator pdf
  p. 6, Eqs. (2)–(3); the Crossref title was confirmed). Supporting rows are TE-051 and TE-054
  (Xiao et al. 2019, 10.1016/j.joule.2019.02.006), TE-046 (Ong et al. 2008, O2-open, 10.1021/cm702327g)
  and TE-055 to TE-058 (Li–Al–O windows). The PDFs are in W1's local corpus, not in git.

## Schema differences for Day 2

**Against W4** (`configs/preprint/results_schema.json` at `cc50fc0`):

1. The provenance block conforms, and is validated in every run. The other parts do not fit.
2. W4's `regime` and `link` and `quantity` definitions describe Monte Carlo screen output (draws,
   seed, sobol_design, links anchor/sequester/nucleate, quantiles). The Pourbaix outputs are
   deterministic grids and windows with no place there. W4 needs a C5 section (for example
   `links.stability`, or a deterministic-field definition) if the screen is to read them.
3. W4's `prior_file` and `prior_file_hash` hold the run configuration here, because the
   Pourbaix runs have no prior file.
4. W4's root has `additionalProperties: false` and `schema_version: "1"`. The Pourbaix files
   carry extra roots (`provenance_pourbaix`, `inputs`, `session`) and are not W4 documents.
5. The window limits that the screen consumes need a status field (`finite` or `undetermined`).
   W4's `quantity` has none, and an undetermined limit must never be read as a pass.

**Against W6** (`figures/src/schemas/fig7_pourbaix.schema.json`, assumed-v0):

1. `regimes` requires exactly two (pH, E) panels. R-cell has no pH axis; it is a per-phase
   window in V vs Li+/Li. The export holds R-slurry only, and W6 needs a window-panel type for
   R-cell.
2. `domains[].phase` allows only `solid` and `aqueous`. Multi-element domains are mixtures
   (for example `Al(OH)4[-] + CaCO3(s)`), and 5 of the 8 mock domains are `mixed`.
3. `species` is a single joined label. The component list is in `slurry.json`
   (`domains.polygons[].components`) but not in the export.
4. `potential_range` has no reference electrode. R-slurry uses V vs SHE, and R-cell would use
   V vs Li+/Li.
5. Fig 7 has no field for decomposition energies, the ACC-excluded region or the carbonate
   speciation. W6 must add one if the figure should show any of them.
6. These fields conform: `mock`, `schema`, `note`, `provenance`, `conditions`, `ph_range`,
   `polygon` and `water_lines` (two end points per straight line).
