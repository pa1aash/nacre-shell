## Case design (physics first)

**A case is one pairwise, same-study comparison.** A test surface is set against the study's reference surface (`SCHEMA.md`). Each case is binary, so the coin-flip null is exact per case.

**Selection is outcome-blind (`SCHEMA.md`, section 2).** The reference is:
- the authors' control;
- otherwise the carboxyl SAM;
- otherwise chitosan.

Each other surface with a directional outcome gives one case.

**Two physical constraints shaped the set.**

1. **No circular inputs.**
   - γ_net or α′ fitted from the same ln J against σ⁻² slope is the outcome restated, so it is never an input.
   - Usable inputs are therefore measured or computed independently of the rates:
     - DFS binding free energies (Hamm 2014; Nielsen 2020);
     - simulated interfacial energies (Hu 2012);
     - OWRK contact-angle surface energies (Giuffre 2013, SI Table S2);
     - near-surface Ca²⁺ by ion-selective electrode (Deng 2015, Fig. 2);
     - MD interaction energies (Yu 2023).
   - Studies with only fitted γ_net were excluded: Knight 2024, the B28 peptoid, and the GISAXS α′ studies.
2. **Barrier, not rate.**
   - A rate orders barriers only if the kinetic prefactors are equal.
   - Nielsen 2020 shows the reverse: B28 has the lowest B but the slowest J₀ at σ = 5.73. Giuffre 2013 shows the same inversion.
   - So the slope B is used wherever reported. Rate, count or mass readings carry a declared common-prefactor assumption.

**Contract mapping.** Inputs enter `theory/contract.yaml` as registered:
- Young–Dupré, cos θ = W/γ_nl − 1;
- Δγ_w = 0 for in-water free energies;
- the Ω channel for enrichment.

## Criterion summary

**Per-case verdict.**
- **Correct.** The central 90 % interval of Δ = ΔG*_het(test) − ΔG*_het(ref) excludes zero on the observed side.
- **Incorrect.** The interval excludes zero on the opposite side.
- **Inconclusive.** The interval contains zero.

An inconclusive case stays in N and counts as not correct. A conclusive-only tally is reported as descriptive only.

**Pass rule.** k is the smallest integer with P(X ≥ k | N, ½) ≤ 0.05.
- N = 12 and k = 10.
- P(X ≥ 10) = (66 + 12 + 1)/4096 = 79/4096 = 0.0193.
- k = 9 would give 299/4096 = 0.0730.
- `nacre retro check` recomputes these from `cases.yaml` and checks that `criterion.md` matches.

**If the rule is not met.** Each failing case is diagnosed: dominant input, flagged assumption, boundary mass, and whether the inputs or the reading are at fault. The frozen result is reported regardless, with no tuning. Any change makes a new criterion version with a deviation entry.

**Limits stated before the run (`criterion.md`, section 5).**
- **Not independent.** The cases share references and studies, so 0.0193 is nominal. A study-level tally is reported.
- **One direction.** All observed relations are `test_higher_barrier`, a consequence of the reference rule. A label-swap symmetry check is required.
- **Correlations already published.** 11 of 12 sources themselves report the input–outcome correlation. Those cases test the mapping and sign conventions (SF1), not new physics.
- **Weakest cases.** RC11 (common θ assumed) and RC12 (MD energies; deposit mass).

## Contrary and novelty updates

- **N4 (B13, material): chitosan is not dissolved in a strongly alkaline slurry.**
  - The sources: pK about 6.5, acid-soluble only; alkali is the standard way to precipitate it (Younes and Rinaudo 2015; Aranaz 2021, full texts).
  - Soluble routes reach about pH 7.4 only (oligomers, half-acetylation). LiOH/urea dissolution needs freeze–thaw and about 2 M LiOH.
  - The templating studies used pre-cast insoluble films: "Chitosan is insoluble at the experimental pH of 10" (Knight 2024, Section 2.4.1), and Giuffre 2013 used electrodeposited films.
  - The slurry is pH 9.7–12.5 (Maffre 2025).
  - Implication: the Introduction must describe a pre-formed chitosan film, or state the constraint. A charged soluble derivative changes the surface chemistry to which the low barrier is attributed.
