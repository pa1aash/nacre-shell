## Q1 and Q2: the result in one paragraph of physics

The template sits in an open interfacial region that exchanges ions with the slurry. At
equilibrium every ion carries the same electrochemical potential at a binding site, in the
diffuse layer and in the bulk. A nucleus at the template is therefore driven by k_B T ln
Omega_bulk.

- **Specific binding** raises the local Ca2+ concentration by exactly the amount it lowers the
  local standard potential: k_B T ln E_enr = -Delta_G_seq/N_A. The two cancel in the driving force.
- **A mean electrostatic potential** enriches Ca2+ and depletes CO3^2- by reciprocal factors.
  Those cancel in the ion product and in the neutral solid, which settles Q2.

The "local-ideal" bookkeeping of contract v1 gave the enriched ions the bulk standard state. In
doing so it assumed the layer is a reservoir held above the bulk chemical potential, and it
credited the binding free energy a second time, as driving force.

In the exact grand potential of a cap, every template-ion interaction is an excess quantity in
the interfacial energies. The Gibbs adsorption equation with the Langmuir isotherm puts the bound
Ca2+ into the template-liquid energy, and only there:

Delta_gamma_ads = -R T (Gamma_s ln(1 + x) - delta_s c_bulk), with x = E_enr c_bulk / c_max.

Under the nucleus the sites hold lattice ions at the bulk chemical potential, so gamma_sn does not
depend on a_Ca. The consequence reverses the v1 sign. At fixed gamma_sl0 and gamma_sn, stronger
binding lowers cos(theta) and raises the barrier, as an adsorbed inhibitor would. Ca2+ binding
favours nucleation only if the template-nucleus energy falls by more than the template-liquid
energy does.

A genuine local excess of driving force needs a non-equilibrium state: concentration during
drying faster than exchange, slow exchange with the reservoir, or local pH excursions. These lie
outside a thermodynamic screen.

## Screen rule (derivation, Section 7, rule)

1. The driving force is Delta_mu = k_B T ln Omega_bulk at the regime activities. E_enr, c_local
   and Omega_local_ideal never enter a barrier.
2. Delta_G_seq enters the barrier only through Delta_gamma_ads, added once to the Ca-free
   gamma_sl0. When gamma_sl is computed at the regime activities, nothing is added; this is the
   same rule as for Delta_gamma_w.
3. gamma_sn is the template-crystal contact energy with lattice ions at the sites. It carries no
   term in Delta_G_seq or a_Ca.
4. Carbonate needs no correction.
5. R-slurry stability is decided at the bulk state. An "enriched" evaluation is a labelled
   non-equilibrium sensitivity.

### Ignorance priors (`9249661`, `theory/ignorance_priors.md`)

**gamma_sn ~ U[0, gamma_p + gamma_nv].**
- The upper bound comes from the dry work of adhesion, gamma_p + gamma_nv - gamma_sn >= 0. The
  lower bound comes from interface stability.
- The wet work is not bounded, because its sign is exactly the non-wetting branch:
  W_wet,sn <= 0 is the same as cos(theta) <= -1.
- Delta_gamma_w cannot be identified separately under ignorance. The prior therefore sits on the
  total, with Delta_gamma_w = 0. If a dry-reference gamma_sn0 is measured, the bound is
  0 <= Delta_gamma_w <= gamma_p - gamma_sl.
- The comparator energies have gamma_ow ~ U[0, gamma_o + gamma_w] and
  gamma_op ~ U[0, gamma_o + gamma_p].

**Branch masses** (G = gamma_p + gamma_nv; checked as exact integrals):
- complete wetting: clip((gamma_sl - gamma_nl)/G, 0, 1);
- partial wetting: 2 gamma_nl/G in the interior;
- non-wetting: clip(1 - (gamma_sl + gamma_nl)/G, 0, 1).

**Deviation from the ruling's wording.** The ruling asked for a demonstration that the prior
gives non-zero mass to all three branches. That holds only when gamma_sl > gamma_nl (complete
wetting) and W_pw + W_nw > 2 gamma_w (non-wetting). The prior on gamma_sn excludes no branch that
the other energies allow. If gamma_sl <= gamma_nl over its support, C4 fails for every
admissible gamma_sn, and that verdict does not depend on the ignorance prior. The file says so
