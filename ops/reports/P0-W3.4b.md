## The physics, in physical terms (A1 to A5)

Derivation: `theory/derivation_body.tex`, Section 7 (Admissibility), with labels th:eq:adm to
th:eq:favourable. SI: `theory/si_derivation.tex`, subsection thsi:sec:admissibility.

- **A1 Stability.** gamma_sl is the work of making unit area of template-water interface. If it were
  negative, the film would lower its free energy by growing more interface: it would swell, roughen or
  disperse until elasticity, crosslinks or anchoring stopped it. The bounded interface that the cap free
  energy and Young's relation assume would not exist. So gamma_sl = gamma_sl0 + Delta_gamma_ads >= 0 is
  necessary at every registered state.
  - Limits: it is necessary, not sufficient, and an ultra-low positive value is admitted.
  - With gamma_sn >= 0 and gamma_nl > 0, every admitted state has three non-negative in-water energies.
  - Its Ca-free case is the cp3 truncation gamma_sl0 >= 0.
- **A2 Reference state.** The Gibbs integral changes only the Ca2+ potential. gamma_sl0 must therefore
  be the same interface without Ca2+ at the regime T, pH and ionic strength.
  - Measured with Ca2+ at c_ref: add only Delta_gamma_ads(c_bulk) - Delta_gamma_ads(c_ref). Adding the
    full term double-counts.
  - Measured in pure water, or estimated from probe liquids on the dry film (the sourced key is of this
    kind): there is no double count, but the pH and ionic-strength terms are missing. By the Lippmann
    relation, which makes the energy largest at zero charge, those terms lower the energy of a film
    charged further from its point of zero charge. The sourced value therefore overstates the
    reference.
  - The term is a small-change expansion about the Ca-free structure. Its validity ratio r_ads =
    -Delta_gamma_ads/gamma_sl0 is reported. Admissibility is r_ads <= 1. Between zero and one the state
    exists, but its gamma_sl carries an error the term does not control.
- **A3 Site versus solution.** The analog is a solution stability constant. With the site-volume
  standard state it fixes the Langmuir odds directly, x = alpha_2 K c_bulk/c_std, and Gamma_s and delta_s
  drop out. Three differences separate a site from the free ligand.
  1. **Ligand form (a required correction).** The constant refers to the catecholate dianion, so the
     cycle must charge both deprotonations: `Delta_G_seq_cycle_2H`. The one-proton cycle overstates the
     binding. The overstatement is largest where the regime pH lies below pKa2.
  2. **Repulsion and competition (no correction needed).** Lateral repulsion within a layer of fixed
     sites and competition by protons and cations only lower the occupancy at every concentration. By
     Pi = R T ∫ Gamma_Ca d ln c, Langmuir at a given affinity is an upper bound on the lowering. The rule
     needs no correction for these effects.
  3. **Geometry and environment (unquantified).** These move the affinity either way by an unknown amount.
     The analog stays conditional on itself. The measurement that removes it is a Ca2+ uptake isotherm of
     the template.

  **The cap.** The lowering is largest where the Gibbs excess vanishes, at c_0 = c_max (1 - 1/E_enr):
  Pi_ads_max = R T Gamma_s (ln E_enr - 1 + 1/E_enr) < -Gamma_s Delta_G_seq. That is at most the binding
  free energy of a full layer of sites. It is independent of delta_s and unrelated to gamma_sl0, so the
  Gamma_s and delta_s bounds do not cap the term below gamma_sl0. Some dose overshoots once
  ln E - 1 + 1/E > gamma_sl0 a_site/(k_B T). The right side is the bare interfacial work of one site
  footprint in thermal units, a fraction of one for a hydrophilic film. Overshoot is the generic outcome
  for such a film binding by more than a few k_B T.
- **A4 Rule.** See "The rule chosen and why".
- **A5 Consequence for C3 and C4.** f <= 1/2 exactly when gamma_sn <= gamma_sl. With both energies
  non-negative, the favourable region is the wedge 0 <= gamma_sn <= gamma_sl. **A favourable template
  requires the crystal-template contact to be at least as favourable as the template-water contact.**
  This is physics and holds under any prior.
  - A binding template has gamma_sl <= gamma_sl0 + R T delta_s c_bulk, so binding narrows the wedge.
  - For C4: S_spread <= gamma_sl - gamma_nl is unchanged. On an admitted draw, theta = pi needs
    gamma_sn >= gamma_sl + gamma_nl. It can no longer come from a negative gamma_sl.

## The rule chosen and why

**Rule (i): restrict the joint prior.** The joint prior of (gamma_sl0, Delta_G_seq, Gamma_s, delta_s) is
restricted to gamma_sl_insitu >= 0.
- **Where.** At every registered R-slurry state and design point. A draw is admissible only if the
  condition holds at every state.
- **Refused draws.** Counted, never clipped and never floored.
- **Retained mass.** M_adm is printed beside every verdict that uses gamma_sl (C3, C4, deletion test).
- **Labels.** Verdicts are labelled "conditional on admissibility".
- **Inadmissible inputs.** If 1 - M_adm >= p_cred, the link verdict is inconclusive, labelled
  "inadmissible inputs", and the restricted verdict is printed beside it.
- **Also reported.** P(gamma_sl_floor >= 0) and the r_ads quantiles.
- **The unrestricted P0-W4.3 run** is a labelled sensitivity.

Why this rule:
- **(i) chosen.** gamma_sl >= 0 is a condition on whether the described state exists, not a law that
  limits the binding. A3 shows the binding can exceed gamma_sl0, and what fails then is existence. This
  is the joint form of the cp3 truncation.
- **(ii) rejected.** A saturating form has no physical basis. The finite site count bounds the lowering
  by Pi_ads_max, not by gamma_sl0. A floor at zero would place refused mass on a boundary state the
  physics excludes. Any particular saturating function would be a fit.
- **(iii) not registered.** The coupling is physical (the same groups set the charge and bind Ca2+) but
  unmodelled. The restriction induces the statistical dependence instead, so the Sobol analysis must
  treat the restricted inputs as correlated (N2).
- **Clipping cos(theta) at -1 also rejected.** It counts a non-existent interface as non-wetting.

**Bookkeeping and sign.**
- The rule restricts inputs and adds no channel. Delta_G_seq still enters once, through Delta_gamma_ads,
  and Omega = Omega_bulk. The no-double-counting checks pass unchanged.
- The admissible set is an upper set in Delta_G_seq (SF4), so the refused draws are the strongest
  binders.
- SF2 holds on the admitted draws: binding alone raises the barrier, and the template helps only if
  gamma_sn falls by more than gamma_sl does.

**Interpretation for cp4.** The "inadmissible inputs" clause goes beyond a bare restriction of the prior.
It is recorded in `theory/cp2_fixes.md` with its reason: at that level the stability condition rejects the
input set at the credibility the decision rule asks of any verdict. It applies to feasible and falsified
verdicts alike.

## Retained prior mass and branch masses (properties of the priors)

**The expressions.**
- M_adm = P_prior(gamma_sl0 + Delta_gamma_ads(Delta_G_seq, c_bulk, Gamma_s, delta_s) >= 0), per design
  point and state.
- Along the analog route, Delta_gamma_ads = -R T (Gamma_s ln(1 + alpha_2 K c_bulk/c_std) - delta_s
  c_bulk).

**How they were evaluated.** By Monte Carlo over the priors of `lit/params.yaml` (400 000 draws, seed
20261019; scratch script, not committed). Inputs:
- the gamma_sl0 key (truncated) and its untruncated sensitivity;
- `gamma_s_site_density`;
- delta_s loguniform on [Gamma_s V_m, d_layer], with the calcite V_m and `template_layer_thickness`;
- `log10_k_ca_catecholate`, `pka_catechol`, `pka2_catechol`, `ph_slurry` and `t_slurry`;
- c_bulk on a design scan, since it has no prior;
