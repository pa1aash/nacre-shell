### Diagnoses (physics first)

**RC04, inconclusive. Cause: the input data, specifically DFS precision; the model is not at fault.**
- Under Dupré, f falls monotonically with W = |ΔG_b|/(N_A A_c). The sign of Δ in every unclipped draw is therefore the sign of |ΔG_ref| − |ΔG_test|. A_c, γ_nl and Ω are shared and cannot flip it.
- The reported DFS energies are 3.6 ± 0.6 kT (amine) and 5.0 ± 0.7 kT (carboxyl). That gives P(|ΔG_ref| > |ΔG_test|) = Φ(1.4/0.92) = 0.936. The run gives 0.937.
- The median is on the observed side. The interval crosses zero only because the measured binding energies overlap at 1.5 σ.
- A-RC04-1 (DFS at pH 8.2, nucleation at pH 9.5) adds uncertainty that the sd values do not carry.
- No boundary mass.

**RC07, RC08, RC09, incorrect. Cause: the input route, contact angles in air converted to γ_sl in water by the OWRK combining rule, together with A-RC07-1 (common γ_sn). The C3 Young relation itself is not at fault.**
- OWRK gives γ_sl = (√γ_s^p − √γ_w^p)² + (√γ_s^d − √γ_w^d)². It is smallest for a film whose components match water's, and it rises for films more polar than water.
- From SI Table S2, the γ_sl values are 12.6 mJ/m² (heparin), 7.9 and 8.0 (LG and HG alginate), 1.8 (hyaluronate) and 5.2 (chitosan).
- So three of the four test films get a higher γ_sl than chitosan. A higher cos θ then means a lower barrier, all conclusively.
- The measured slopes B instead rise with the total γ_s in air (84, 77, 77, 63.5 against 39 mJ/m²) and with the negative charge per sugar (−1.36 to −0.52 against +0.28).
- Hyaluronate (RC10), whose polar component (52.4) is closest to water's, is the one film that OWRK places below chitosan, and it is correct.
- The physical difference between the films is ionic: charge density and Ca²⁺ and water binding in solution. Air contact angles do not see it, and the case assumes it absent from γ_sn (A-RC07-1, which the authors flag as a secondary control).
- Under contract v3, stronger in-solution Ca²⁺ binding would raise the barrier (SF2), the observed direction. No in-solution input exists for these films, so that reading is not tested.
- Boundary mass is at most 0.01. The unapplied OWRK sd (above) could not make these cases correct.
- New contrary entry: **B14**. It bears on P0's own `gamma_sl0_template`, which comes from the same contact-angle route.

**RC11, incorrect. Cause: the model, specifically the single-entry bookkeeping of bound Ca²⁺ (A-RC11-6), together with the missing contact data (A-RC11-1).**
- Under contract v3, Ca²⁺ bound at the template lowers γ_sl through Δγ_ads. COOH holds more Ca²⁺ (4.9 against 2.05 mM near the surface), so it gets the lower γ_sl and the higher barrier (SF2).
- The pipeline therefore predicts CH3 lower, against the observation (no CaCO3 on CH3).
- The sign is independent of Γ_s and δ_s (A-RC11-5), so neither prior choice causes the miss.
- **The magnitude is negligible.** Δ has median −0.15 kT, on barriers near 930 kT. The dilute-limit Δγ_ads ≈ −R T δ_s (c_local − c_bulk) is between 10⁻⁶ and 5 × 10⁻⁴ J/m² over the δ_s bound. A few-fold enrichment cannot switch nucleation through γ_sl.
- **Where the contrast must be.** The observed contrast must lie in γ_sn, the template–nucleus contact.
  - The model books bound Ca²⁺ on γ_sl only, and γ_sn has no data here (RX12), so it is a shared nuisance.
  - Carboxylate–Ca–calcite bridging (Γ_Ca,sn > 0) lowers γ_sn and reverses the sign of d(γ_sl − γ_sn)/dμ_Ca when Γ_Ca,sn > Γ_Ca,sl.
  - The Dupré cases, whose inputs reach γ_sn directly (RC01–RC03, COOH binding calcite most strongly), are all correct.
- Boundary mass 0.0001.
- New contrary entry: **B15**. It gives empirical support for D14, where C2 does not gate.
